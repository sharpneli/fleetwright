# Crews under fire: who held their stations, 1890-1925
Status: draft    Updated: 2026-10-28    Request: [REQUESTS.md "Crews under fire: who held their stations, 1890-1925 first"](../REQUESTS.md) (the plan: [docs/battle/crew.md](../../docs/battle/crew.md))

*This note gathers what the record says about men at guns and other exposed stations under fire, from the Yalu (1894) to 1925, with WWII actions and land, air and civil evidence as checks. It is written for the crew model in crew.md (step N3, decision 5): a pressure per station, a threshold by CrewQuality, a share taking cover unordered, and reliefs walked in by doctrine. The case material comes from ten research notes built mostly on after-action reports, memoirs (McGiffin, Semenov, Kostenko, King-Hall, von Hase), despatches and secondary works that cite Campbell, Tarrant, Staff and others. Many primary texts could not be reached (see Open questions). Where the evidence is thin the note says so, and the numbers proposed for the model are marked as estimates.*

Tags: **[SRC]** sourced (a cited text says it); **[INFERRED]** reasoning from sourced facts, or a fit; **[EST]** an estimate with no direct source; **[UNCERTAIN]** thin, recalled or conflicting sources.

The men in these tables were real, and most of those named died at their stations; the aim here is to get their conduct right.

## Summary

1. **Regular naval crews of 1890-1925, of every training level found, almost never left a working gun unordered.** Across more than 40 cases in six navies, gun stations went silent because the men were killed or wounded, because fire, flash, steam or gas made the place unworkable, because the gun or its supply was wrecked, or because an officer ordered them out. Green and poorly practised crews (Cervera's Spanish, the Beiyang ironclads, Kent's reservists) held to very heavy losses: Vizcaya's batteries fought until "there were no men left" to serve them. Strength: moderate. The pattern is consistent, but the sources (despatches, memoirs, award citations) favour steadiness, and the worst-hit ships left few witnesses. **[INFERRED]**
2. **Unordered flight is recorded in a few clear settings.** These are short local panics after a single shocking blast on a green, demoralised crew under overwhelming HE (Suvorov's signalmen; men near exploding cartridge boxes, who came back after a rally at about 15:40). Others are men driven from open light guns in a beaten squadron (Spanish at Santiago, by US and German observers' account), and civilian crews turning back when lit up and fired on before many were hit (Dardanelles trawlers, 1915). Two more are collective or political decisions (Slava's sailors' committee emptying the engine room, 1917) and captains taking whole ships out of the fight (Jiyuan and Guangjia at the Yalu). **[SRC]**
3. **What set the difference was crew type and leadership more than drill.** Civilian against naval crews in the same boats under the same fire (Dardanelles) was the clearest difference found. So were captains who fled against captains who stayed, and officers present against absent: Semenov's frozen fire parties went to work on a direct order. A wrecked or cleared station also differed from one that held, and so did open guns against armour. Time in commission and practice showed mostly in **accuracy, readiness and weapon failures**, not in staying (Kent "Our shooting was rotten"; Tiger "unprepared and inefficient"; Arethusa's guns jamming). **[INFERRED]**
4. **Ordered cover, not unordered flight, moved casualties most.** At Ulsan (1904) Rossia's captain had the engaged side's quick-firer crews lie down and the unengaged side's go below. Her sister Gromoboi kept hers manned. Gromoboi lost 87 killed against Rossia's 44 (49% fewer dead on Rossia) and 257 against 200 casualties in all (22% fewer). Chester's open-backed 5.5-in guns took about 4.6 casualties a hit at Jutland, against about 0.4 a hit for Kent's casemated cruiser at the Falklands, roughly 11 times as many (different shells, so not a clean pair). **[SRC]** figures; **[EST]** ratios.
5. **Re-manning ran on three clocks.** Within a crew it took seconds: the next man stepped into a killed gun captain's place and fired (Zhenyuan; the 1866 USN drill thinned a 16-man crew to 6 by dropping the highest numbers). Moving whole idle crews took minutes (Zhenyuan's disabled 6-in crew asked for orders and went to the 12-in; Queen Mary's X turret crew to the 4-in; Scharnhorst's forward turret crews carried shells for Caesar). Rebuilding a ship's guns from stokers or "unskilled chaps" needed a lull (Southampton kept dark "to reorganize"). Shifting control after a disabling hit took about 5-10 min (Marlborough reopened fire 9 min after her torpedo). **[SRC]** No clock-timed case of a gun crew killed and replaced by men from elsewhere was found. **[UNCERTAIN]**
6. **The relief pool was small.** "There are no spare men on board a warship" (Semenov). Reliefs came from crews ordered under cover (Suvorov's 47 mm crews), the crews of wrecked or idle mounts, and stokers once speed was lost (Southampton, Blücher, Emden). Taking men from fire parties cost the ship its fires (Vizcaya). The one written relief rule found is the USN's 1899 station bill: excess gun numbers held as a reserve in the Powder Division, with an engine-room section as "2nd relief". **[SRC]**
7. **No navy's written order on cover for 1890-1925 was read.** Practice shows the RN at Jutland with its anti-torpedo battery only partly manned (Colossus: "two Starboard 4-in. guns ... manned"), 6-in casemates manned where they could reach (Tiger, Barham, Malaya), and bridges and tops manned in the open in every navy. The Russians in 1904-05 kept light-gun crews under cover until a torpedo-attack signal. **[SRC]**
8. **No measurable "under fire" effect on accuracy or rate was found beyond casualties, damage, smoke and visibility.** Training and fire-control method moved hit rates by 2.5-4.5 times (Thunderer 67% against Orion 15% in the same sea; the Grand Fleet line 3.6% against Beatty's battlecruisers 1.4%). Heavily hit ships kept hitting, and lightly engaged ships often missed. Von Hase, unengaged, shot "as at gun practice". Land and police data put the pure being-shot-at effect at about ×0.6 on hits for individual shooters. That transfers weakly to director-controlled ships. **[SRC]** data; **[INFERRED]** transfer.
9. **For the model:** keep thresholds high and the green-to-elite spread narrow (about 2.5× in pressure) for regular crews at gun stations. Let quality act mainly through recovery time, the share that leaves when the threshold is crossed, and the officer effect. Build pressure from casualties at and in sight of the station, penetrating hits, fire and fumes, and near bursts at open stations; hit totals are a poor guide (Lion: 14-16 hits at Dogger Bank, 1-2 killed). Add a straddle and illumination term only for civilian and green crews. Add ship-wide overrides for committee and civilian crews. Add no new gunnery factor. **[EST]**

#### 1.6 Three narratives that carry a mechanism

**Suvorov: freezing, local panic, hiding and the rally.** Semenov saw three different things on one ship. Men stayed at the fire mains but did nothing, "as if mesmerised, gazing at the smoke and flames", until he gave short direct orders. A group that had been calm (the signalmen, "still feeling indispensable to the fight") broke for seconds after a shell burst among them and swept him along. Men near exploding cartridge boxes fled, and "men who had been hiding came back to work" after a cheer at about 15:40 ([Semenov](https://gutenberg.org/files/57324/57324-h/57324-h.htm)). His own model of nerve: a stupor that turns into either "uncontrollable panic" or unusually high spirits. **[SRC]** Semenov wrote partly to defend the squadron, and he was a staff officer, not a station officer. For the model these are three states, not one: present but not working until an officer acts; a few seconds' to minutes' rout after one blast; and hiding ended by a rally event. **[INFERRED]**

**Rossia and Gromoboi: the price of ordered cover.** Same class, same squadron, same day, same enemy, different captains' orders. Rossia's engaged-side quick-firer crews lay down and her unengaged side's went below; Gromoboi kept hers manned. Gromoboi had 87 killed and 170 wounded; Rossia 44 and 156 ([Wikipedia, Gromoboi, citing Brook, *Warship 2000-2001*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_cruiser_Gromoboi)). **[SRC]** Deaths fell by about half and wounded by 8%, so the men removed were the most exposed ones, and Rossia's engaged-side light guns were silent while their crews lay down. Rossia's hit count is not known, so the pair is not fully controlled. Gromoboi got six more 6-in guns in light casemates afterwards. **[EST]** for the ratios. This is the period's best anchor for the captain's posture order.

**Southampton: casualties, not flight, and the lull.** At about 1,500 yd under four cruisers' guns and searchlights, 75% of her upper-deck men were hit in 3.5 minutes. King-Hall wrote that the guns stopped "all through lack of flesh and blood". Afterwards the guns' crews were "practically non existent". She re-manned them with stokers off watch and kept dark because "we simply had to have time to reorganize" ([King-Hall](http://www.vlib.us/wwi/resources/northseadiary.html)). **[SRC]** Casualties at about 25 a minute emptied the stations, the reliefs came from the engineering department, and they came only when the fire stopped. **[EST]**

### 2. What set the difference

**Crew type outweighed drill.** The sharpest contrast in the record is civilian against naval. The Dardanelles trawlers turned back on being lit up and fired on, while the destroyer Wear and the picket boats carried on under the same guns, and battleship sailors in the same trawlers were "much more willing" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_operations_in_the_Dardanelles_campaign)). **[SRC]** Among naval crews the poorly practised held: Cervera's men had "no target practice" in peacetime and still fought the guns until no men were left ([Eulate](https://spanamwar.com/vizcayaeulate.html), [Proceedings 1899](https://www.usni.org/magazines/proceedings/1899/april/sketches-spanish-american-war)). Coronel's reservists kept up fire for an hour and Monmouth refused to surrender. Kent's reserve crew held its guns and shot "rotten". **[SRC]** Training shows up in accuracy, not staying. **[INFERRED]**

**Revolutionary and mutinous crews.** Slava's committee emptied the engine room after 7 hits and 3 killed, while her guns and damage control worked ([Wikipedia, Slava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_battleship_Slava)). The breakdown was selective, collective and driven by fear of sinking. The HSF 1917-18 and Cattaro 1918 mutinies were refusals to sail on idle big ships, not failures under fire ([Wikipedia, Wilhelmshaven mutiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelmshaven_mutiny); [Cattaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattaro_mutiny)). **[SRC]**

**Time in commission and work-up.** New ships held but worked badly: Chester (3-5 weeks), Tiger ("quite unprepared and inefficient", Beatty, Nov 1914; 2 hits from 355 rounds at Dogger Bank), Arethusa (guns jammed), the Samar escorts (under a year, and they held). The RCN found nearly half its sea breakdowns in men's first 6 months at sea ([Am J Psychiatry 1945](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/ajp.101.5.625)). **[SRC]** Commission time reads as a gunnery and weapon-failure input. It has only a small effect on station-keeping. **[INFERRED]**

**Officers and petty officers present or lost.** Officers restarted stalled stations (Semenov's fire mains; officers dragging men from Suvorov's lower battery). They worked last guns themselves (Emden, Shark, Sparrowhawk). They decided when a station was cleared (Queen Mary's X turret) and when ships were beached (Oquendo "after consulting the officers"). **[SRC]** Losing them did not stop guns (Vizcaya's lower battery after Ristory; Akagi through three commanders; Oryol through all her turret officers wounded). It did move control and, on the Spanish ships, preceded the decision to beach. **[SRC]** Rowland's land data put "heroes" strongly with rank, with awards per man killed of 1.56 for officers, 0.52 for senior NCOs and 0.10 for other ranks ([Themself.org on Rowland](https://www.themself.org/2013/12/stress-of-battle-5-ww2-heroism-surprise/)). **[SRC]** The officer is the strongest single steadying factor the evidence supports. **[INFERRED]**

**Armour and cover.** Armour changed who was hit far more than whether men stayed. Open and shielded guns drew the killing: Chester's legs lost under open-backed shields; Malaya's and Barham's casemates burned out by flash; Colossus's top and bridge men hit; San Francisco's secondary and AA crews "suffered especially". Turret crews kept working until a penetrating hit killed them together (Derfflinger C and D, Lion Q). **[SRC]** Kostenko adds that ordered cover moved Oryol's losses rather than ending them: most fell to splinters through gun ports, sighting slits and casemate ports ([Kostenko ch. XLIV](https://militera.lib.ru/memo/russian/kostenko_vp/44.html)). **[SRC]**

**Fire, fumes and steam made stations untenable on their own.** Laiyuan's broadside guns "could not be manned, being surrounded by flames". Derfflinger's transmitting station was abandoned on order for gas. Bruno's turret on Scharnhorst filled with propellant gas each time the breeches opened. Emden's starboard engine room lost 18 of 20 men to scalding steam. Suvorov's engine rooms suffocated as the ventilators drew smoke in. **[SRC]** These are physical limits, not morale. **[INFERRED]**

**Casualties seen around a station.** No source isolates this. The cases that broke (Suvorov's signalmen, the men by the cartridge boxes) broke at a blast among them. The cases that held after seeing it (Cornwell; Zhenyuan's loading step; Southampton) were drilled crews with a task. **[INFERRED]** Mueller & Pietsch's suppression model has men who recently ducked ducking again ([NPS 1978](https://archive.org/stream/humanfactorsinfi00muel/humanfactorsinfi00muel_djvu.txt)), and the Blitz near-miss work finds survivors close to a blast more afraid afterwards ([Standing Well Back on MacCurdy](https://www.standingwellback.com/near-misses-lessons-from-ww2-and-a-danger-highlighted/)). **[SRC]**

**Having a task.** Men with something to do held. Men waiting froze or hid: Suvorov's fire parties before orders, and the lower battery's "about fifty men" in a stupor once their guns were wrecked. **[SRC]** for the cases; **[INFERRED]** for the mechanism (Dollard's veterans rated training 45% and leadership 49% as what replaced fear: [Time 1943](https://time.com/archive/6789396/army-navy-whos-afraid/)).

**Earlier action and duration.** Neptune's crew found their first action "exhilarating and beneficial" ([1st BS reports](https://battleofjutlandcrewlists.miraheze.org/wiki/Reports_from_First_Battle_Squadron)). In Dollard's survey of Spanish Civil War volunteers, 64 of 100 said they were less afraid the more often they went in ([Time 1943](https://time.com/archive/6789396/army-navy-whos-afraid/)). Wear-out takes weeks and months (the RCN data, the Okinawa pickets, Appel's 200-240 days for infantry). That is outside a single battle. **[SRC]**

**Readiness, which is not courage.** Savo's cruisers were wrecked in Condition II with exhausted men. Hercules' men had "fallen out to get tea" and closed up "on hearing gun-firing". **[SRC]** Surprise and fatigue decided more there than any threshold could. **[INFERRED]**

**Hit totals are a poor guide.** Lion took 14-16 heavy hits at Dogger Bank for 1-2 killed. Invincible took 22 hits at the Falklands with none killed. Carmania took 79 hits for 9 dead, and Blücher about 11 killed a hit. Tiger's officer thought they were "not being hit very often" while she took 17 hits in about 40 minutes ([Wikipedia, Dogger Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Dogger_Bank_(1915)); [warhistory.org](https://warhistory.org/article/the-battle-of-the-falkland-islands-iii?amp=0); [Fawcett & Hooper](https://archive.org/details/fightingatjutlan00fawcuoft)). **[SRC]** Pressure has to come from what lands near the men. **[INFERRED]**

The distinction the model needs is between a station emptied by casualties, physical causes or orders, and one emptied by men leaving. ### 4. Re-manning and reliefs

**Within the crew: seconds.** On Zhenyuan a man "on the step below" took the killed gun captain's place, "corrected the aim, and fired" ([McGiffin](https://navyandmarine.org/ondeck/1894YaluBattle.htm)). The USN *Ordnance Instructions* (1866) carried tables thinning a 16-man crew to 14, 12, 10, 8 or 6, dropping the highest numbers first while the rest took on their jobs ([Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/19058/19058.txt)). The War Office 1925 12-pdr manual replaces casualties by "named successors" within a 7-man detachment, and gear a casualty was using is "left by him on the gun floor" ([QF 12-pdr manual](https://nqht2020.duckdns.org/wp-content/uploads/QF-gun-manual.pdf)). **[SRC]** In-crew renumbering is not a relief in the model's sense. It is the station working short-handed, with the least skilled duty dropped first. **[INFERRED]**

**Change of control: about 5-10 minutes.** Marlborough was torpedoed at 18:54. With every turret's firing generator flooded she "Opened fire" at 19:03, by director from the fore-top ([1st BS reports](https://battleofjutlandcrewlists.miraheze.org/wiki/Reports_from_First_Battle_Squadron)). Warspite stopped ten minutes to repair her steering, then fought with A turret in local control ([Wikipedia, Warspite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Warspite_(03))). Princess Royal shifted control to B turret when the Argo tower was knocked out. San Francisco's control went from the bridge to Battle II to the conning tower within the action (WDR 26). **[SRC]** These shifts use the men who are already there. **[INFERRED]**

**Idle or wrecked mounts' crews: minutes.** Zhenyuan's disabled 6-in crew "came into the barbette and asked for orders" and went to the 12-in. Queen Mary's X turret men were offered to the 4-in guns on the PO's suggestion. Scharnhorst's forward turret crews carried ammunition aft for Caesar, and Lützow's gun crews brought up shells from flooding magazines. **[SRC]** WWII continuity: on Salt Lake City, "turrets shall provide reserve AA crews" when no main-battery condition was set ([memoranda 1941-42](https://ussslcca25.com/memorandums-41-42.htm)). **[SRC]** No written source names the disengaged side's crews as reserves, though Rossia sent hers below at Ulsan. **[UNCERTAIN]** for a rule; **[SRC]** for the practice on one ship.

**Crews held under cover: the first reserve.** Suvorov's 47 mm and machine-gun crews, sent below the armour before the fight, "were used to fill gaps". Oryol's light-artillery crews waited in the battery deck for the torpedo-attack signal ([Semenov](https://gutenberg.org/files/57324/57324-h/57324-h.htm); [Kostenko](https://militera.lib.ru/memo/russian/kostenko_vp/38.html)). **[SRC]** On a flagship under concentrated fire that pool was used up within hours. Its size on a battleship was perhaps 40-80 men. **[EST]**

**Stokers and idle hands: late, and for supply first.** Southampton re-manned her guns with "stokers off watch". Blücher's stokers carried ammunition once she had lost speed. Emden's last gun was worked by "the Artillery Officer and a few unskilled chaps". The USN 1899 bill rotated an engine-room section into the Powder Division as "the 2nd relief". **[SRC]** Stokers become free when the ship slows. Untrained reliefs fire at a fraction of the rate: Emden had one gun of five firing with about half the crew disabled, about 20% of her broadside. **[EST]**

**Damage control and steering as sources and sinks.** Vizcaya drew fire-party men to the guns, and the fires then got out of hand once the mains were cut. Reina Cristina drew her steering crews from the guns, and the guns ran down to two men. **[SRC]** Every relief costs the station it came from. **[INFERRED]**

**The nearest man of the fitting trade.** When Heidkamp collapsed at Seydlitz's second flooding valve, the Feuerwerker Müller, the ammunition artificer stationed nearby, finished the work ([de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMS_Seydlitz)). **[SRC]**

**The pool and its size.** "There are no spare men on board a warship" ([Semenov](https://gutenberg.org/files/57324/57324-h/57324-h.htm)). In WWII the Okinawa pickets began 18% under complement and got no replacements; Moosbrugger thought a 100% reserve the minimum ([Waddell 2025](https://www.usni.org/magazines/naval-history/2025/june/more-beer-and-better-movies-managing-resiliency-picket-line)). In short night actions reliefs never caught up: San Francisco had five 5-in guns intact and unmanned. **[SRC]**

**Moving under fire.** The WWII USN rules were to "proceed on double to your station and stay there", to send turret crews up through the shell decks rather than over the weather deck, and for AA reliefs to "wait for lull before relieving, taking advantage of nearby cover" and "not interrupt fire of gun when relieving" ([Salt Lake City](https://ussslcca25.com/memorandums-41-42.htm)). The 1866 rule: no man leaves his quarters on the pretext of helping the wounded. **[SRC]** Southampton's reorganisation also waited for the fire to stop. **[SRC]**

### 6. Rates of fire and accuracy under fire

**Training and method dominate.** In rough weather in 1912 Thunderer (director) hit with 26 of 39 rounds and Orion (independent laying) with 4 of 27: 67% against 15% ([Naval Gazing, Directors](https://www.navalgazing.net/Directors)). At Jutland the Grand Fleet battle line hit with 3.6% and Beatty's 1st and 2nd BCS with 1.4%, and the line had the Scapa range while Rosyth had none ([Wikipedia, citing Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damage_to_major_ships_at_the_Battle_of_Jutland); [jutland1916.com](https://www.jutland1916.com/tactics-and-technologies-4/gunnery-performance)). Japanese 12-in hit 4.7% at the Yellow Sea and 9% at Tsushima, the page crediting the shorter range ([navweaps](https://navweaps.com/Weapons/WNJAP_12-40_EOC.php)). **[SRC]** These factors of 2-4.5 are far larger than anything a nerve effect would need to explain. **[EST]**

**Being hit did not visibly ruin shooting.** Lützow, Moltke and Tiger kept scoring while hit in the run to the south. New Zealand, hit once, and Tiger made 6 hits in 723 rounds, five of them Tiger's ([jutland1916.com](https://www.jutland1916.com/tactics-and-technologies-4/gunnery-performance)). **[SRC]** The per-ship split (New Zealand about 0.24%, Tiger about 1.65%) rests on recalled round counts. **[UNCERTAIN]** Lion hit with about 1.5% at Jutland and about 1.6% at Dogger Bank. **[EST]** from [Campbell via Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Lion_(1910)).

**The one qualitative testimony.** Von Hase, left unengaged for about ten minutes by a British distribution error, engaged "with complete calm, as at gun practice", with "continually increasing accuracy" ([Wikipedia, Derfflinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMS_Derfflinger)). **[SRC]** The likely mechanism is that spotting is easier with no enemy splashes around one's own ship, rather than steadier hands. **[INFERRED]**

**Mechanical causes of the fall-off.** Emden's fire slackened "very quickly" as rangefinders, voice pipes and the foremast control went, before heavy casualties. Oryol found squadron fire "impossible" and went to group fire after half an hour; soot on the sights and smoke stopped her after turret. Bismarck's fire went "increasingly erratic" once both control stations were gone. **[SRC]** At Santiago the Americans hit with 1.3% (122 of 9,433), or 3.5% for major calibres (42 of 1,200), under almost no return fire. The causes named are sights set for over 10,000 yd at 6,000 yd, smoke, and "stress" in passing ([Naval History 2013](https://www.usni.org/magazines/navalhistory/2013-03/we-must-expect-disaster); [NHHC H-Gram 020](https://www.history.navy.mil/about-us/leadership/director/directors-corner/h-grams/h-gram-020/h-020-6-victory-at-santiago-.html); [Naval Gazing](https://navalgazing.net/Spanish-American-War-Part-8)). **[SRC]** Stirling's "under one hit in fifteen" probably counts heavy guns only. **[UNCERTAIN]**

**Rate.** The battle rate was usually capped by the spotting cycle, not by loading: Derfflinger's 385 main rounds over 8 guns is about 0.35-0.5 rounds a gun a minute if she fired for 100-140 minutes, 12-16% of her 3 rpm. **[EST]** Japanese 12-in combat rates were 0.2 (Fuji) to 0.5-0.75 (Mikasa) a minute ([navweaps](https://navweaps.com/Weapons/WNJAP_12-40_EOC.php)). Under fire at short range, Roberts' two 5-in guns fired about 8.6 rounds a gun a minute for 35 minutes. **[SRC]** A US writer in 1925 thought short-range practice "somewhat more hectic and feverish than a battle would be" for loaders ([Proceedings 1925](https://www.usni.org/magazines/proceedings/1925/september/possible-improvements-our-gunnery-training)). **[SRC]**

**WWII air defence.** The 1945 AA summary blames rising rounds per kill on more ships firing, night, faster planes, extreme-range doctrine and too little training, never on nerves. Gunners "improved in accuracy as a result of increased experience in action" ([AA Action Summary](https://history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/a/antiaircraft-action-summary.html)). 5-in VT took 200 rounds per kamikaze against 550 per ordinary attacker, the opposite of what a big "under attack" penalty would predict (geometry explains it). **[SRC]**

**What an under-fire factor could be.** If one is wanted at all: a small multiplier on layer and trainer error under high station pressure, falling with quality and halved under director control, plus a spotting penalty from enemy splashes near one's own ship. The evidence equally supports no factor, provided men leaving, damage to control, smoke, spray and visibility are modelled. **[EST]**

Measurable inputs for setting a ship's level **[INFERRED]**:

1. Crew new to the ship, under 6 months in commission, or more than 30% reservists and recruits: down one for gunnery, none to one for steadiness.
2. Six months or more of fleet work-up: up one for gunnery.
3. Live firing about 4 rounds a gun a quarter (Grand Fleet 1915-17) counts as trained. A few poor practices on a voyage (Russia 1905) counts as green.
4. Combat experience: up one for steadiness.
5. A long-service PO core: in 1906, 52% of USN POs held continuous-service certificates against 16.3% of the force.
6. Morale drain from long voyages, idle fleets, food and mail: down one for steadiness only.

## Recommendations for this codebase

The plan's shape fits the record: pressure per station, fading; thresholds by quality; a share leaving; men under cover as the relief reserve; all through where men stand. The numbers below are **[EST]** unless marked; they are fitted to the cases above, not measured.

**R1. Keep thresholds high and the spread narrow.** For regular naval crews the record shows unordered leaving at gun stations as rare at every training level, so most of the quality difference should go into recovery, share and officer effect, not into the threshold. Proposed thresholds Θ in the pressure units of R2 (1.0 = the whole station's crew hit at once):

| CrewQuality | Θ (threshold) | Share leaving when well over Θ, open station | Same, enclosed (turret, casemate, below armour) | Return delay after P falls below 0.6 Θ | Pressure decay speed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| civilian (auxiliaries) | 0.10 | 0.6 | 0.4 | 10-30 min, or not that sortie | ×0.5 |
| green | 0.30 | 0.35 | 0.15 | 4-8 min | ×0.67 (slower) |
| average | 0.45 | 0.20 | 0.08 | 2-4 min | ×1.0 |
| trained | 0.60 | 0.10 | 0.04 | 1-2 min | ×1.2 |
| elite | 0.75 | 0.05 | 0.02 | 0.5-1 min | ×1.5 (faster) |

The civilian row is not a `CrewQuality` today (green, average, trained, elite). It would be a fifth level, or the ship-wide override of R6 setting these numbers for auxiliaries; the other four rows map onto the existing enum.

Make the share a smooth function of P (for example a logistic centred on Θ with width 0.15 Θ, scaled by the share above), not a switch. Leave a hard core that never goes on its own: share caps of at most 0.6 for civilians and lower for naval crews. That is Rowland's "heroes" (about 20%) plus naval discipline. The green-to-elite threshold ratio of 2.5 follows the land evidence that elite effects are modest **[SRC: Rowland]**. Men who go on their own take cover at the nearest cover (0.2, prone) and are **not** in the relief pool until they return. That is the Suvorov "hiding" state.

**R2. What the pressure is made of.** Build it from what happens at and in sight of the station, not from ship hit totals.

| Event | Adds to P | Half-life | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| A man of the station killed or wounded | 1/n of the station (n = men at it) | 4 min, with 30% held until the wounded are taken away | The main term; Southampton, Vizcaya, Chester |
| The station's officer or senior PO hit | +0.15 extra | 4 min | Plus R4 |
| Casualties in sight at a neighbouring open station | 0.3 × their share | 4 min | Open stations only |
| Penetrating hit inside the station | +0.30 | 90 s | Suvorov's turrets; Oryol's casemates |
| Flash or fire in the station | +0.40 while it burns | 2 min after it is out | Separately, the space can be untenable (R7) |
| Near burst, splinters through (within about 15 m, open) | +0.08 | 30 s | Multiply by 0.6 shielded, 0.4 casemate, 0.3 turret or below armour (collocation and parapet effects, [SRC: SASE II, SUPEX]) |
| Blast knocks the crew down | no P; men unable to work for 10-60 s | n/a | Colossus No. 5 gun |
| Fumes or gas in the station | Work rate falls (masks, coughing); P +0.05 a minute while present | 2 min | Derfflinger; Bruno |
| Straddle on own ship, or lit by searchlight | +0.04 per salvo | 30 s | **Civilian and green only**; Dardanelles trawlers; McGiffin's "at first the strain was intense" |
| Rally event (officer arrives, cheer, order given) | −0.15 and return delay reset to 0 | n/a | Suvorov 15:40; Semenov's fire parties |

Sensitisation: each time a station goes over Θ, lower its Θ by 10-15% for the rest of the action, recovering half over 30 min (near-miss and re-duck evidence, [SRC: MacCurdy; Mueller & Pietsch]). Do not add a term for hits elsewhere on the ship: the Tiger perception case and the remote-miss finding argue against it. The decay speeds in R1 scale all half-lives. All half-lives are per game second, so the same pressure arises at any time compression.

**R3. A pre-fire term only for the lowest grades.** The Dardanelles case is the one where men turned back before taking casualties, and they were civilians. Apply the straddle and illumination term of R2 to civilian crews at full weight, to green crews at half, and not to the others. No sourced case shows trained naval crews breaking before being hit. **[SRC]** for the case; **[EST]** for the weights.

**R4. Officers and senior POs.** While the station's officer or senior PO is present and unhurt, multiply Θ by 1.25 and the share by 0.5, and send men out only on order, in good order (Queen Mary X). When he is hit, multiply Θ by 0.8 and the return delay by 1.5-2 until a relief officer arrives (from a junior-officer pool walking in like any relief). For green crews with no officer present after a penetrating hit or fire, mark a share of the men (0.2-0.4) as **present but not working** (Semenov's "mesmerised" men). They count as absent from the station's work, take casualties where they stand, and go back to work at once on a rally event. This keeps "all through where men stand" while capturing freezing. **[EST]**, anchored on [SRC] Semenov, Queen Mary X and Rowland's rank effect.

**R5. Contagion: none, or very small.** No quantified source was found, and collocation and the panic literature point the other way. If one is wanted, add at most +10% to the share once the first men go, only when no leader is present. **[EST]**

**R6. Ship-wide overrides, outside the station model.**

- **Committee crew** (a scenario or period flag, e.g. Russia 1917): when flooding is reported or the list passes about 5°, stations below the waterline empty, whatever their local pressure, and the captain's orders to them are not obeyed. Guns and damage-control parties carry on (Slava). **[SRC]** case; **[EST]** trigger.
- **Civilian auxiliary crew:** civilian thresholds (R1) with the pre-fire term, and abandonment when the enemy warns, unless a bold skipper commands (Otranto; Gowan Lee). **[SRC]**
- **Captain's flight and surrender** stay with the captain's temper and fall-out rules, not with crew pressure (Jiyuan, Guangjia, Nebogatov). **[SRC]**
- **Campaign morale** (long voyages, idle fleets) sets CrewQuality before the battle, steadiness only (2nd Pacific Squadron; HSF 1917-18). **[INFERRED]**

**R7. Physical untenability as its own state.** Fire, flash, steam, gas and flooding should empty or slow a station directly, through the fire and flooding models: Laiyuan's broadside, Emden's engine room, Bruno. Do not route this through pressure, because men leave a burning compartment whatever their quality. Men forced out this way join the nearest cover and count as reserve. **[SRC]** cases.

**R8. Posture: the captain's cover order.** Keep the bold/timid split. The record gives one clean in-period pair, Rossia (cleared) against Gromoboi (kept manned): about half the deaths. The model's cover factor of 0.2 for prone men, against full exposure at open guns, should reproduce a halving of a ship's deaths when the open light guns' crews are a large part of those killed. Check this against the Rossia/Gromoboi yardstick before tuning. Put **"when to man the secondaries"** in doctrine as a field, with values Always / In range of a worthwhile target / Torpedo-craft threat only. Suggested period defaults:

- **1890-1904:** Always (the open-mount era; the Chinese and Spanish crews were exposed by default).
- **1904-1918:** casemate 6-in batteries "in range" (Tiger, Barham, Malaya fired); open 3-4-in batteries "torpedo-craft only", with a watch of two guns a side manned (Colossus).
- **1918-1925:** as 1904-1918.

A timid captain applies the stricter rule a step early, and a bold one keeps guns manned (Gromoboi). Bridge, tops, look-outs and searchlights stay manned in every period (Colossus, Beatty's staff). **[SRC]** practice; **[EST]** defaults.

**R9. The relief order against the record.** The default order is mostly supported. Specific points:

- **In-crew succession first, and it is not a relief.** Renumber within the crew in seconds and drop the least skilled duties (1866 thinning tables; the 1925 12-pdr manual). **[SRC]**
- **Crews of wrecked or idle mounts early: supported** (Zhenyuan, Queen Mary X, Scharnhorst, Lützow, Salt Lake City). **[SRC]** Disengaged-side crews: supported as practice (Rossia), not as a written rule. **[UNCERTAIN]** for a rule.
- **Men under cover by posture are the first reserve: supported** (Suvorov, Oryol). **[SRC]**
- **Stokers off watch: supported, and they become free when speed is lost.** Tie their availability to the engines' state (Southampton after the action; Blücher at low speed). **[SRC]**
- **Damage-control men above a floor: supported, with a cost** (Vizcaya lost her fires). Keep the floor. **[SRC]**
- **"A live mount's handling room is never stripped": make it a doctrine option, not a law.** The USN 1899 bill held the reserve in the Powder Division and drew reliefs from it. Offer ReliefOrder variants: *HandlingRoomProtected* (default) and *ReserveInSupply* (USN 1899: excess gun numbers wait in the supply parties and go first; idlers refill the supply). **[SRC]**
- **Main before secondaries unless torpedo craft threaten:** not contradicted. Reina Cristina moved men from guns to steering, so allow steering and the conn to outrank guns. **[SRC]** case.
- **Reliefs wait for a lull.** A relief bound for a station whose pressure is over the relief party's own Θ should wait at cover, or go and take casualties on the way if ordered (USN 1941-42 "wait for lull"; Southampton). **[SRC]** for the practice.
- **Reliefs' skill.** Men from other trades work at green gunnery level for that weapon (Emden's "unskilled chaps"). A crew rebuilt from mixed reliefs loses 10-20% of its rate for the rest of the action. **[EST]**
- **ReliefFloorShare.** 0.2-0.25 of a station's crew is the only sourced figure, and it is a watch floor, not an action floor (Salt Lake City: three men a manned 5-in). Default 0.25 for stokehold and damage-control floors. **[EST]**

**R10. ReliefWalkS per section.** Use the times in section 4's table, all estimates. They are 5-15 s within a crew, 20-60 s between adjacent guns, 1-2 min within a section, and 3-6 min end to end below the armour deck. A whole crew taking over another turret takes 5-15 min before first fire. For doctrine by period, keep these per period only if the walking routes differ (closed armoured hatches from about 1905). Otherwise one set will do. **[EST]**

**R11. No new gunnery factor.** The existing GunneryCrews rows already carry quality in rangetaker and layer error and in the battle factor on reload. Add nothing on top. The decline should come from men leaving and working short-handed, damage to directors, tops, voice pipes and rangefinders, smoke and soot on sights, spray, and visibility. The sources found give no measured nerve effect, and every quantified gap they do give is explained by training, method, range or visibility. **[SRC]** for that pattern. If playtests want one, the most defensible small term is error ×1.2 green, ×1.1 average, ×1.05 trained, ×1.0 elite on layers and trainers. It should scale with station pressure and halve under director firing. A second option is a spotting penalty from enemy splashes near one's own ship (von Hase's "as at gun practice"). **[EST]** Separately, raise weapon-failure rates for ships under about 6 months in commission (Arethusa, Tiger). **[SRC]** cases.

## Open questions

1. **What did the written orders say on cover and reliefs?** The Grand Fleet Battle Orders (TNA ADM 116/1341-1343; the Dreadnought Project pages when reachable) cover the anti-torpedo crews, men not at quarters, and fire and repair parties. Makarov's chapters on the crew in battle are in the 1990 Naval Institute Press reprint of *Discussion of Questions in Naval Tactics* (ISBN 0-87021-779-8). Fullam & Hart, *Text-Book of Ordnance and Gunnery* (1903), vol. 2 ch. XXXIV, and the USN *Ship and Gun Drills* (1905, 1914) hold the US drills. The German *Gefechtsrolle* and *Leckwehr* organisation are in Groos and Staff.
2. **How long did it take to re-man a gun from elsewhere, and with how many men?** No clock-timed case was found. Sources: Campbell, *Jutland: An Analysis of the Fighting* (per-ship reports, rounds and casualties); the 1920 *Official Despatches* ships' reports in full (archive.org `battleofjutland300grearich`); Walwyn's narrative of Warspite; Tiger's and Lion's reports; Seydlitz's war diary via Staff.
3. **What did Oryol's crews do minute by minute?** Kostenko's full battle narrative (the 2004 edition, «Орёл» в походе и в бою) would show which turret crews went below and who re-manned them. Read it against Novikov-Priboy's *Tsushima*, which has a Soviet slant, and Campbell's Tsushima analyses in *Warship* (1978), which give per-ship hits and ammunition.
4. **How did crews behave aboard the wrecks?** Sources: Zenne's account of Wiesbaden; the KENNEDY FW 6 Blücher survivor interrogations (King's College London); Pochhammer on Gneisenau; von Mücke and Hohenzollern on Emden; Melnikov and Jessen's report on Rurik; Rudnev's report on Varyag; Lundgren, *The World Wonder'd*, on Samar.
5. **How much fire made men take cover, and how long until they came back?** Rowland's book itself (the reviews say breakpoints and recovery are "given no numerical values"); Copp's ORS 2 material in *Montgomery's Scientists* (the interval from a barrage lifting to the defenders' fire); Stouffer, *The American Soldier* vol. 2; Swank & Marchand (1946) on wear-out curves; Janis (1951) on habituation to bombing.
6. **Did ships shoot worse when hit?** Ship-by-phase hits per gun-minute at Jutland, set against the hits each ship was taking, would settle whether any under-fire factor exists. Sources: Campbell's rounds per ship; Brooks, *Dreadnought Gunnery and the Battle of Jutland* (2005).
7. **What were the US 1898 ships' practice scores against their action results?** The Bureau of Navigation's 1898 appendix (ships' reports, the hit board's tables).
8. **What were the period's practice allowances and service terms?** Rounds per gun per year before 1914 for the RN, Germany, Japan, Russia and the USN; the share of reservists in Good Hope, Monmouth and the 2nd Pacific Squadron.

Access note: archive.org, Google Books, HathiTrust, militera.lib.ru (for the later passes) and the Dreadnought Project wiki were largely unreachable in this research. Several primary texts named above were therefore not read, and some claims rest on summaries or on Wikipedia articles citing Campbell, Tarrant, Staff and others. Quotes fetched through a summarising tool are near-verbatim, not checked against page images.
