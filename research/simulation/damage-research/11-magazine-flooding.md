# 11 — Flooding a Magazine: How Long, and Who Gave the Order, 1890-1925
Status: draft    Updated: 2026-11-05    Request: [REQUESTS.md, 2026-11-05 "Flooding a magazine"](../../REQUESTS.md)

## Summary

- **The rules of the period give times. They aren't only inferred.**
  - The USN's 1917 *Naval Ordnance* says every flood pipe and magazine sprinkler "must be of sufficient size to fill the magazine in 20 minutes" [SRC].
  - French ships of the *Saint Louis* class took 30 min to flood their after 12″ magazines, because the water came in at the bottom [SRC: USNI 1911].
  - *Leonardo da Vinci*'s flooding "was not completed in 20 minutes" [SRC: Admiralty Technical History 1919].
  - No valve bores were found. Valve physics puts the USN's rule at a **100-250 mm** valve for a 50-300 m³ magazine (§2.3, [INFERRED]).
  - **Full: 15-30 min by period and design (centre 20). Bottom third wet: about a third of that.** Above the waterline, the fire main is the only source, at a few m³/min.
- **Valves were locked, and keys or spanners were needed to open them.**
  - **RN:** a locked deck-plate on the main deck, worked with a spanner, plus a second locked station below "in case one was untenable owing to fire" (Attwood) [SRC].
  - **USN:** a deck plate on the berth deck. Its key lay in a locked rack, and that rack's key was kept "with the magazine keys". The magazine keys were "kept in the captain's cabin" (1917) [SRC].
- **Who decided:** whoever saw it first and still lived. Three people ordered Lion's Q magazine flooded independently:
  - Harvey, down the voice pipe;
  - the Chief Gunner, at the magazine 2-3 min after the hit;
  - Chatfield, from the bridge on the sergeant's report.

  At the Dogger Bank, Seydlitz's turret crews all died. The order came from her executive officer, the ship's damage control chief.
- **Who opened the valves:** often engine-room men, not gunners.
  - Seydlitz: the second pump master Heidkamp and a *Feuerwerker*, in smoke, turning red-hot valves.
  - Liberté: the chief engineer and two master machinists.
  - Imperatritsa Mariya: an engineer midshipman.
  - Jutland: an acting stoker petty officer (CGM).
- **The decider dying was rarely what failed. The valve station was.**
  - Every recorded battle flash that stopped short of the magazine, 1894-1916, was followed by flooding or by something that did the same job (a shut door, a hose, the fire burning out). Of 13 cases with records, 7 were flooded on order; none had an order given and then failed.
  - The failures are fires that took the valve flat with them: Liberté, Glatton, Benedetto Brin and Imperatritsa Mariya (part), with Leonardo da Vinci flooding too slowly. Of 8 accidents with minutes to spare, flooding saved 3 (Missouri, Suffren, Mississippi) and failed or came too late in 5.
  - In battle, Good Hope (~40 min ablaze) and Black Prince (≤15 min ablaze) blew up after minutes on fire. Whatever was tried left no record.
- **The game today** orders flooding after every flash that stops short, 120 s later, and fills the magazine in 600 s.
  - **The order** is about right as a median when someone outside the train survives, and the record says they nearly always did.
  - **The fill is too fast**: 20 min (1,200 s) fits the period. It should slow after half (Torricelli), as the game's 600 s run on Lion's Q already does by chance.
  - **What's missing:** the valve station as a place the fire can take, and the keys.

## 1. What the valves were, by navy

### 1.1 Royal Navy (Attwood, *War-Ships*, 1904-1917 editions, approved for H.M. ships' libraries) [SRC]

- **The water's path:** magazines (and, in recent ships, shell rooms) are flooded "direct from the sea".
  - "Usually one sea valve supplies a number of magazines."
  - There's a second valve at the magazine. Both are "locked valves, which must be deliberately released."
- **Controls:**
  - The valves "can be operated either from the main deck or below, the alternative position being provided in case one was untenable owing to fire. Both positions are 'locked.'"
  - Above: "an ordinary deck-plate worked by a spanner".
  - Below: a handwheel loose on its spindle. A padlocked cotter pin has to be shifted before the wheel turns the valve.
  - Handwheels and plates are marked L, "and must only be opened under proper instructions."
- **Air escapes:** a lift valve on a pipe led well above the magazine crown.
- **Dry dock:** a pipe from between the Kingston and the magazine to the upper deck for dockyard hoses; in later ships, a fitting on the Kingston itself.
- **Draining:** "No means are provided for draining magazines."
- **Pumps:** in a "recent large ship", one 50 t/h motor pump per section, its 4-inch sea suction taken off a magazine flood.
- **Lion's Q:** one technical author on the Harvey talk page has "spindles from the main deck, with a local position on the deck above" [SRC, secondary].
- **Glatton, 1918:** her "upper positions for flooding" lay inside the after superstructure. They were "inaccessible after the explosion". The Technical History drew the lesson: "access from the weather deck to the upper positions" [SRC: CB 1515 (24)].
- **After Vanguard (1917):** the remedies included "fitting drenching arrangement" in magazines [SRC: CB 1515 (24) §95(B)(b)]. So **the RN had magazine drenching (sprinklers) by 1918**. That corrects note 10 §7/V6, which had none before the late-1920s USN.

### 1.2 US Navy (*Naval Ordnance*, USNA text, 1917, ch. XVIII) [SRC]

- **The water's source:**
  - Rooms below the waterline: a sea valve.
  - Rooms above it: the fire main, with relief valves "to prevent the accumulation of a pressure that would make the bulkheads collapse".
- **Pipes:**
  - Shell rooms: one plain opening.
  - Powder magazines: "many small holes on the underneath side, and is called a sprinkler."
  - Sprinklers were also over the gun-loading positions in turrets and the powder-loading positions in handling rooms.
  - **"In every case the pipe, both flood-pipe and sprinkler, must be of sufficient size to fill the magazine in 20 minutes."**
- **Pre-dreadnoughts:** a valve "worked either in the magazine itself or from the berth-deck". The deck spindle takes "a large key". It is "kept near at hand in a locked rack, the key of which is kept with the magazine keys. In some cases the socket … is locked by a padlock … Magazine keys are kept in the captain's cabin."
- **Later ships:** flooded by groups from one valve, with "cut-out valves [that] can be operated only from the magazines".
- **Tests:** the pipe is tested to a cap, and a bucket shows the water. Sprinklers can't be tested without wetting the magazine.

### 1.3 Germany

- **Seydlitz, Dogger Bank:**
  - The flood valves for the after turrets' magazines were in **Abteilung III**.
  - Heidkamp went into the "already heavily smoke-filled" compartment.
  - He opened them with the help of the *Feuerwerker* Franz Müller [SRC: de.wikipedia, after von Mantey 1921 and Staff].
  - The valves were "glowing red-hot" [SRC: en.wikipedia].
- **The water:** it ran in through the vent trunks too, and the stern settled 1.05 m (note 10 §4.3).
- **Not found:** German valve sizes, control stations and written rules.

### 1.4 France, Italy, Russia, Japan

- **France:**
  - Liberté's flood valves lay beneath the magazines [SRC: Dawlish], with locks on the berth deck for the amidships group. Heat may have bent the operating gear [SRC: USNI 1911].
  - Officers called the arrangements "a challenge to common sense".
  - After 1911 all ships' flooding and cooling were tested, and constructors were sent to study British methods [SRC: USNI 1911]. The commission found the flooding arrangements "insufficient"; the navy made only "modest improvements" [SRC: en.wikipedia, after Jordan & Caresse].
- **Italy:** Leonardo da Vinci, 1916: "a quick method of flooding the magazines and drenching the ammunition while flooding is going on" was the lesson. She had no "spraying arrangements" [SRC: CB 1515 (24)].
- **Russia:** Imperatritsa Mariya, 1916: the magazines were flooded by hand, "but some could not be reached because of the fire" (Kolchak's telegram) [SRC: AiF].
- **Japan:** nothing on the valves was found. The Navy Minister's 1919 statements mention cooling machinery and "another apparatus", which the Admiralty read as drenching [SRC: CB 1515 (24) §99].

## 2. How long

### 2.1 Figures from the period

| Source | Ship or rule | Time | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| USN *Naval Ordnance* 1917 | every flood pipe and magazine sprinkler | **full in 20 min** (a design rule) | [SRC] |
| USNI 1911, after the Liberté inquiry | *Saint Louis* class, after 12″ magazines | **30 min**, water in at the bottom | [SRC] |
| Technical History 1919 | Leonardo da Vinci | "flooding was not completed in 20 minutes"; "almost completed" when the unflooded top layer went | [SRC] |
| Imperatritsa Mariya | the forward shell magazine | flooded by Ignatyev's party before the ~40-min explosion; others flooded "at once" | [SRC] |
| Lion's Q, Jutland | from the hit at 16:00 | doors shut in 0-3 min; flooding "later"; enough water by 16:28 that the buckled doors held | [SRC] |

### 2.2 The order and the walk

| Case | Decided by | How it travelled | Valves | Time to water |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Missouri 1904 (turret flash to handling room) | Capt. Cowles | the bridge's order | not given; Monssen shut himself in the magazine and "nearly drowned" | minutes [SRC] |
| Seydlitz, Dogger Bank, 10:40 | the I.O. (executive officer) | to the pump masters | Abt. III, in smoke, red-hot | not timed; in time [SRC] |
| Lion's Q, 16:00 | Harvey (turret officer); also the Chief Gunner; also Chatfield | voice pipe to the handing room ("close the magazine doors and open the magazine flood valves"); the gunner in person; the bridge | spindles to the main deck | doors 0-3 min; flooding "later" [SRC] |
| Derfflinger C, 19:13 | not recorded | — | — | "timely closing and flooding" [SRC] |
| Liberté 1911, 05:17-05:53 | Lt Bignon (fire quarters); Lt Garnier took over and **countermanded** flooding the central and after magazines | orders to the chief engineer, Lestin ("I've already tried") | forward: driven back by smoke; amidships: locks on the berth deck, three men at them when she blew | failed over 36 min [SRC] |
| Mississippi 1924 | not recorded | — | — | "quick action in closing and flooding the magazines" [SRC] |

What it comes to, by step [EST from the cases]:

- **Decided:**
  - 0-1 min by a survivor in or near the train (Harvey, Willons at the doors, a handing-room crew);
  - 2-4 min by the gunner or the DC officer, on smoke or a messenger (Lion's gunner at 2-3);
  - 2-6 min by the bridge, on a report (Chatfield's order was "forestalled").
- **Carried:** a voice pipe or telephone is seconds; a messenger is 1-3 min.
- **Valves opened:**
  - clear station, key at hand: 0.5-2 min;
  - key in a rack or the captain's cabin: 2-5 min (the USN's keys);
  - a station in smoke, got through: 3-15 min (Seydlitz);
  - otherwise not at all (Liberté, Glatton).

`magazine_flood_ref.py` §5 sums these to **0.5-3 min** for a survivor in the train with the station clear, and **3-20 min** from the bridge through smoke.

### 2.3 Valve physics against the rule [INFERRED]

A sea valve fills through a falling head (Torricelli): Q = c·A·√(2gh). Take c 0.4-0.6 and a magazine 3 m high with its floor 7 m below the waterline (`magazine_flood_ref.py`).

| V, m³ | bore for the USN's 20 min (c 0.5) | a 150 mm valve: ⅓ / ½ / full, min | a 250 mm valve: ⅓ / ½ / full, min |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | 100 mm | 2.8 / 4.3 / 9 | 1.0 / 1.5 / 3 |
| 100 | 145 mm | 5.6 / 8.5 / 18 | 2.0 / 3.1 / 7 |
| 200 | 205 mm | 11 / 17 / 37 | 4.0 / 6.1 / 13 |
| 300 | 250 mm | 17 / 26 / 55 | 6.0 / 9.2 / 20 |

- **The second half takes as long again as the first, or longer.** Full takes 2.1-2.4 times as long as half, more so for a shallow magazine.
- **Above the waterline only the fire main fills it.** At the rule's 20 min a 100 m³ room needs 5 m³/min (1,300 gpm), which is a large share of a 1917 dreadnought's fire pumps. So upper magazines were small, or filled more slowly.
- **The air escapes also set the rate.** Shutting them stops the fill (Blutarski, NavWeaps forum, unsourced).
- **What the water reaches matters as much as the fill:** bottom entry wets the lowest tier first, which is what Leonardo da Vinci died of. A sprinkler (USN 1917, RN 1918) wets the top of the stow within a minute or two of opening.

## 3. Who gave the order, and what failed

### 3.1 Battle: flashes and fires that stopped short of the magazine

| Case | Fire where | Magazine | Who / how | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Matsushima, Yalu 1894 | battery, ready charges | saved, not flooded | a petty officer kept the fire off | [SRC, note 10] |
| Fuji, Tsushima 1905 | exposed charges in the after barbette | saved; nothing recorded on flooding | fire put out | [SRC] |
| Kent, Falklands 1914 | casemate flash down the hoist into the passage | saved | Sgt Mayes threw out a burning charge and "flooded the compartment" with a hose | [SRC: CGM citation] |
| Seydlitz, Dogger Bank 1915 | both after turrets, 62 charges | **flooded** | the I.O.; Heidkamp and Müller | [SRC] |
| Blücher, Dogger Bank | ammunition fire, two turrets out | unknown | — | [UNCERTAIN] |
| Lion, Dogger Bank | A turret lobby fire, quickly out | **partly flooded on a false report** | — | [SRC] |
| Lion's Q, Jutland | gunhouse, working chamber; flash at 16:28 to the magazine doors | **doors shut and flooded** | Harvey, the Chief Gunner and Chatfield; the magazine crew found dead "with their hands still clutching the magazine door handles" | [SRC] |
| Tiger, Jutland | ready cordite in a passage | **midships 6″ magazines flooded** | — | [SRC: USNI 1925] |
| a Jutland ship (CGM to Wherry) | fire near the 6″ magazine | **flooded** "at great risk" by an acting stoker PO | — | [SRC] |
| Seydlitz, Jutland 16:55 | after superfiring turret, 4 charges; fire spread down toward the magazine | saved; flooding not recorded | — | [UNCERTAIN] |
| Moltke, Jutland 17:16 | casemate charges; "burned the ammunition hoist down to the magazine" | saved; flooding not recorded | — | [UNCERTAIN] |
| Derfflinger C, Jutland 19:13 | all exposed charges in the turret | **closed and flooded** | — | [SRC] |
| König, Jutland | 15 cm casemate charges | **flooded** | — | [SRC] |

Of the 13:
- **7 flooded on order** (counting Lion's partial flooding at the Dogger Bank, which came on a false report);
- **3 saved by other means** (a fire put out or held off, a hose);
- **3 unrecorded.**

None records an order given and failing. That is survivorship: a failed attempt in battle left the ship, and the record, gone.

### 3.2 Battle: ships that burned for minutes, then blew up

| Case | Burning before the magazine went | What's known of flooding | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Borodino, Tsushima | a 12″ hit under the forward 6″ turret lit ready-use ammunition (~19:30); she was already on fire from 19:04 | none | [SRC; interval UNCERTAIN] |
| Good Hope, Coronel | forecastle alight from the first salvos (~19:05); "topsides all aflame" by 19:42; forward magazine at 19:50 | none; all hands lost | [SRC] |
| Black Prince, Jutland | "blazing furiously" after several minor detonations; sank within 15 min of the first salvo | none; all hands lost | [SRC] |

**These are the failures the user's question is after.** In each, something like 10-40 min passed in which a flooding order could have saved her. That something failed, whether the order, the walk or the valves, and no one is left to say which. Against §3.1's saves, that is **3 lost against 7-10 saved, or about 1 failure in 3-4 where time existed.** The armoured cruisers and Borodino burned in their batteries and passages, not in one turret's train, which is the case the game's rule covers.

### 3.3 Accidents with minutes to spare

| Case | Minutes from fire to the big explosion | Flooding | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Missouri 1904 | a turret flash, then the handling room | ordered by the captain; done | **saved** |
| Mikasa 1905 | a fire aft, then the magazine (minutes; detail not found) | not found | lost [UNCERTAIN] |
| Iéna 1907 | — | in dry dock: no sea | lost |
| Liberté 1911 | 36 (05:17 to 05:53) | three tries; driven back by smoke; an order countermanded; three men at the berth-deck locks when she blew | lost |
| Suffren 1911 | a bunker fire against the magazine bulkhead | magazine and bunker flooded | **saved** [SRC: press] |
| Benedetto Brin 1915 | fire, then the after magazine | "nor could the magazines be flooded" | wrecked |
| Natal 1915 | 4 | — | lost |
| Leonardo da Vinci 1916 | 25 (23:15 to 23:40) | "begun at once"; not done in 20 min; the top layer went | lost |
| Imperatritsa Mariya 1916 | 5-10 to the first; ~40-57 more to the end | forward shell magazine flooded by Ignatyev's party (killed); after magazines flooded; some unreachable | lost |
| Capitan Prat 1916 | — | vented through the hoist; no flooding needed | saved |
| Glatton 1918 | — | forward magazines flooded; after controls in the burning superstructure | scuttled after 2 h |
| Mississippi 1924 | a turret fire | "quick action in closing and flooding" | **saved** |

Of the 8 where flooding had time and was tried or wanted, it saved 3 (Missouri, Suffren, Mississippi) and failed or came too late in 5 (Liberté, Benedetto Brin, Leonardo da Vinci, Imperatritsa Mariya, Glatton aft), plus Mikasa unknown. **Every failure is the valves, the keys or the slow fill; none is "no one gave the order."** Two involve the order itself: Liberté's countermand, and Lion's own sequence (doors first, flooding "later").

### 3.4 Is a flash that stops short reliably followed by flooding?

**In the record, yes, when it is one turret's or casemate's train.**
- The deciders were plural and mostly outside the train: the gunner, the DC officer, the bridge.
- Whole turret crews died at Seydlitz (159), Derfflinger C (73 of 78) and Lion's Q, and the magazines were still flooded.

**What made it unreliable was place, not people:**
- the valve station in the same smoke as the fire (Seydlitz got through; Liberté didn't);
- locked keys (USN, RN; peacetime ships);
- one station only (Glatton's in a superstructure);
- bottom-entry pipes too slow for a fire already against the bulkhead (Leonardo da Vinci, *Saint Louis*);
- no sea (Iéna in dock).

**One caution for the game:** Lion's Q was saved, by the 1918 Grand Fleet Gunnery and Torpedo Orders' own reading, by the shut doors rather than the water. "Orders were given for the magazine doors to be closed and later for the magazine to be flooded" [SRC, quoted on greatwarforum]. Midshipman Woodhouse heard the order to flood Q magazine passed from the TS "at 5.0", an hour after the hit [SRC, disputed]. So Lion doesn't time the fill.

## 4. Recommendations for this codebase

For [docs/battle/magazines.md](../../../docs/battle/magazines.md) "Flooding and sprinkling", `Magazines.Light`, `Magazines.Valves`:

1. **`FireMagazineFloodS` 600 → 1,200 s** [SRC: USN 1917 rule; *Saint Louis* 30 min; Leonardo da Vinci >20 min].
   - Range 900-1,800 by period and design: pre-dreadnought bottom entry 1,800, a 1917 rule ship 1,200.
   - Keep it per design if shipgen sizes the valve: a bore of 0.1-0.25 m for 50-300 m³ (§2.3).
2. **Fill by head, not linearly**, below the waterline: the rate ∝ √((floor depth − water depth) / floor depth).
   - Full then takes ~2.2× the half time, as the 2026-11-05 measurement already shows.
   - Cells above the waterline fill only from the section's main, at the main's share. The game does this today, and it's the USN's rule.
3. **`MagazineOrderS` 120 s stands as the median when the valve station is clear** [EST from §2.2]. Draw it from 30-360 s.
   - Then add the station: +0 when its cell is clear.
   - When its cell is in fire or smoke: either fail, or +180-900 s at a chance to get through. The record's ~1 in 2-3 (Seydlitz in; Liberté and Imperatritsa partly not) suggests 0.3-0.5 [EST].
   - The fire's own order (B2) and the flash's order should both check the station. Today only the fire's checks the fire over the valves.
4. **Give the valves a place.** One station: the cell above the magazine on the main deck (RN) or berth deck (USN). Optionally a second one below (RN "alternative position") or a remote one (post-war).
   - A design option "flood controls: one / two / remote" carries Glatton's lesson.
   - Lost power doesn't stop hand stations; it stops remote ones only (Astoria, WWII).
5. **Who survived: don't key the order to the train's crew.** Order the flood if any of these hold:
   - anyone in the train lives (voice pipe, 0-60 s);
   - the magazine's own crew live (they shut the doors and flood; Lion's died at the handles 28 min later);
   - the ship is conned (the gunner, DC or the bridge, 120-360 s).

   A ship with no one conning (the bridge gone, DC wrecked) gets the slow path, `CounterfloodHandS`, as today. That is closer to the record than "without fail", and nothing in the record supports "never".
6. **Sprinklers by navy and year:** `MagazineSprinklers` true for the USN by 1917 (powder magazines, handling rooms, turret loading positions) and the RN from 1918 (post-Vanguard drenching). False before, and for the others until shown otherwise. This corrects note 10 §7/V6.
7. **Keys in peace:** at anchor or in harbour, add 2-5 min for the keys (USN: captain's cabin; Tsukuba's key was mislaid). In dry dock, no sea (Iéna); dockyard hoses only.
8. **Countermands:** doctrine may refuse to flood the magazines it doesn't see burning (Liberté's Garnier). A doctrine field `FloodMagazines: own-train / section / all-threatened` would carry it; the default is own-train.
9. **Yardstick:** add §3 to `damage-history`'s magazine lines.
   - In battle, a stopped-short train should be flooded in ≥80% of runs, and the magazine lost in ≤10%.
   - A general fire in a battery or passage should reach a magazine in some runs (Good Hope, Black Prince, Borodino).
   - An accident fire over the valves should be lost about half the time.

## 5. Open questions

- **Valve and pipe bores**: none found for any navy. Friedman's *British Battleships*, Brown's *Grand Fleet*, the RN's *Naval Magazine Regulations* (1917-18 revision) and the USN's General Specifications would have them.
- **Written rules on who may order flooding**: the KR&AI 1913 articles reached say only that the valves are locked, "opened only under proper instructions", and that the gunner checks them. The German, Japanese and Russian rules were not reached.
- **The Jutland cases' details** (Seydlitz's and Moltke's magazines, Malaya, Derfflinger D, Wherry's ship) are in Campbell's *Jutland* and Staff, neither reachable here.
- **Mikasa 1905, Georgia 1907, Matsushima 1908**: whether flooding was tried.
