# F. RN electric plants and turret hydraulic power, 1890-1925 (and to WWII): gap-filling pass
Status: draft    Updated: 2026-11-04    Request: [REQUESTS.md, 2026-11-04](../../REQUESTS.md); an appendix of [electric-plant.md](../electric-plant.md)

Scope note: this pass extends existing notes (Hansard 1901 on Renown/Barfleur/Centurion; Nelson 750 hp steam pumping engine per turret; 15-in Mk I 2 deg/s train, 5 deg/s elevation; Defence 1908 first 220 V ring main; Marlborough's Jutland switchboard trip; Dreadnought's Siemens sets). Many class pages (Wikipedia Hood, Nelson, Iron Duke, Queen Elizabeth, Revenge, Lion, Indefatigable) were checked and say nothing on dynamos. The Dreadnought Project class pages for Queen Elizabeth and Hood came up in search for "dynamos" but could not be fetched (site error), so they may hold figures this pass missed.

## 1. Recorded dynamo plants (number x kW, voltage, drive, placement)

### Takeaway
Hard figures were found for Dreadnought (1906), Invincible (1908), Tiger (1914), Renown (1916), Hood (1920, number and drive only) and Prince of Wales (1941). From about 1913 the pattern was four DC sets of about 200 kW (roughly 750-800 kW in all) feeding a 220 V ring main, with steam reciprocating, turbo and diesel/oil drives mixed so the plant did not depend on one prime mover. By KGV (1939) this had grown to eight 330 kW DC sets (2,640 kW). Pre-dreadnought, Orion, KGV (1911), Iron Duke, QE, Revenge, Lion, Indefatigable, Nelson and the cruiser and destroyer figures were not found.

### Cited findings
- **Dreadnought (1906):** "three 100 kW, 100 V DC Siemens generators", "powered by two Brotherhood steam and two Mirrlees diesel engines" "(which later changed to three steam and one diesel)". A 100 V DC and a 15 V DC system powered "five lifts (elevators)", "eight coaling winches, pumps, ventilation fans, lighting and telephone systems" (citing Johnson & Buxton p. 164 and Brown p. 27). [UNCERTAIN] The article gives three generators but four prime movers. That matches the "three or four" already in the notes and is still unresolved. — [Wikipedia, HMS Dreadnought (1906)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Dreadnought_(1906))
- **Invincible (1908):** "Her electrical power was provided by four 200 kW turbo-generators and motor generators with capacity of 100 kW" (citing NavWeaps' 12"/45 Mark X page). The large figure probably reflects her all-electric turrets (section 2). Voltage not given. — [Wikipedia, HMS Invincible (1907)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Invincible_(1907))
- **Tiger (1914):** "Four direct current electric dynamos with a total capacity of 750 kilowatts" "supplied the common ring main at 220 volts" (Roberts 1978, *Battlecruisers*, p. 89). The split by drive is not given. — [Wikipedia, HMS Tiger (1913)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Tiger_(1913))
- **Renown and Repulse (1916):** "two reciprocating steam-driven 200-kilowatt dynamos", "one oil-driven 150-kilowatt dynamo, and one turbine-driven 200-kilowatt dynamo" "that supplied the common ring main at 220 volts" (Raven & Roberts, p. 50). That is 750 kW in all. — [Wikipedia, Renown-class battlecruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renown-class_battlecruiser)
- **Hood (1920):** eight dynamos, "widely separated": "two driven by diesel engines, two by turbo engines, and four by reciprocating engines". kW and voltage are not stated. This is a contemporary account (Journal of the American Society of Naval Engineers, vol. XXXII, c. 1920). — [hmshood.org.uk, ASNE Journal extract](https://www.hmshood.org.uk/reference/written/asnejournal32.htm)
- **Prince of Wales (KGV class, 1941):** "Out of eight generators (six 330-kw DC turbo-dynamos and two 330-kw diesel dynamos), only three turbo-dynamos were operational when the second torpedo attack commenced at 1219" (p. 26). This gives **330 kW, not 300 kW**: 8 x 330 = 2,640 kW DC. There was a turbo-generator in Y Action Machinery Room and diesel dynamos No. 6 (just aft of B Engine Room) and No. 8, plus a Port Diesel Dynamo Room. The report gives no voltage. — [Garzke, Dulin & Denlay, *Death of a Battleship*, 2012 update (pacificwrecks.com PDF)](https://pacificwrecks.com/ship/hms/prince-of-wales/death-of-a-battleship-2012-update.pdf)
- **Voltage history:** Portsmouth Dockyard history says Portsmouth Yard's own 1892 dynamos were 400 A x 80 V, each driven by a 56 ihp open compound engine at 560 rpm. It also says a committee found electricity in use for "lighting, fans, after capstans, the latest 6-inch turret designs, and ammunition supply, coal, ash and boat hoists" at "80-100 volts". That committee recommended raising the voltage to 200-230 V, adopting a ring main, more dynamos, developing a turbo-generator, and electric drive for all auxiliaries outside the engine room. The page dates this committee **1920**. [UNCERTAIN] That date cannot be right: 220 V ring mains were already in Defence (1908, per the existing notes), Tiger (1914) and Renown (1916). The committee was more likely c. 1902-1905, which [EST] fits the move from Dreadnought's 100 V to Defence's 220 V. — [Portsmouth Royal Dockyard Historical Trust, "15. Electrics"](https://portsmouthdockyard.org.uk/chips15.html)
- The same page says that in 1892 "motors for guns and ammunition supply were not yet considered reliable", while motors for training searchlights had tested well. — [Portsmouth Dockyard, "15. Electrics"](https://portsmouthdockyard.org.uk/chips15.html)

### Inferences
- [EST] Dreadnought-era plants grew from about 300-400 kW at 100 V (1906) to about 750 kW at 220 V (1913-16) to 2,640 kW (1939-41). Hood's eight sets were probably 200 kW each (about 1,600 kW), as in Renown, but no source confirms it.
- [EST] Mixing reciprocating, turbo and diesel drives (Dreadnought, Renown, Hood) meant some generation survived when steam was lost. Prince of Wales shows the limit: in 1941 both diesels stopped through flooding and shock, not lack of steam.
- [EST] Iron Duke had a lower power board in a "diesel engine room" (existing notes, Marlborough). So at least one diesel dynamo per ship was usual in the battle fleet of 1914-16, as in Dreadnought and Renown.

### Gaps
- No figures were found for Royal Sovereign, Majestic, Canopus, Formidable, Duncan, King Edward VII, Lord Nelson, Bellerophon, St Vincent, Orion, KGV (1911), Iron Duke, Queen Elizabeth, Revenge, Invincible's voltage, Indefatigable, Lion, Nelson, or KGV's voltage. Wikipedia class pages are silent, and the Dreadnought Project class pages failed to load. Burt (*British Battleships of WWI*), Raven & Roberts and Roberts (*Battlecruisers*) are the likely print sources; Roberts p. 89 (Tiger) and Raven & Roberts p. 50 (Renown) show the format.
- No cruiser (Town, Arethusa, C class) or destroyer plants were found.
- No published 1906-1916 electrical load list was located. MacLaren (1961) and Scott (1983) were not reached online.

## 2. Turret hydraulics: pumping engines, drive, pressure; any electric-dependent heavy turret before 1930

### Takeaway
RN heavy turrets of 1890-1939 trained, elevated, rammed and hoisted by hydraulics. The ring main was fed by separate pumping engines (Hood had four), and the medium is described as water with soluble oil. The one firm exception is **Invincible**. From 1908 to early 1914 she had four experimental all-electric 12-in turrets (two by Vickers, two by Armstrong). They failed and were converted to hydraulic in 1914 for £151,200, so she fought at the Falklands and Jutland with hydraulic turrets. By KGV (1939) the 14-in elevation was still hydraulic, and the training "swashplate engines" (160 bhp quad, 70 bhp twin) were hydraulic motors. No source found says whether her hydraulic pumps were steam- or electric-driven.

### Cited findings
- **Hood:** "Four hydraulic pumping engines are provided for working the turrets", kept well inboard with their feed tanks. Power, drive and pressure are not given. — [ASNE Journal extract, hmshood.org.uk](https://www.hmshood.org.uk/reference/written/asnejournal32.htm)
- **15-in Mk I (QE, Revenge, Renown, Hood):** "hydraulically powered from a ring main using a water/soluble oil mixture"; "Elevation was by hydraulic cylinder connected to the slide". Hood's Mk II had pneumatic run-out instead of hydraulic and forced lubrication on the training worm shaft. The Mk I/N rebuilds (QE, Valiant, Warspite, Renown, 1930s) redesigned the elevation cylinders and walking pipes and added pneumatic run-out. The page does not say whether the rebuilds got electric-driven pumps. — [NavWeaps, British 15"/42 Mark I](http://www.navweaps.com/Weapons/WNBR_15-42_mk1.php)
- **Renown, WWII:** Lt Peter Elvin reported that the "15-inch gun training hydraulic engines" stopped working at list angles "far less than the designed failure angle". — [NavWeaps, 15"/42 Mk I](http://www.navweaps.com/Weapons/WNBR_15-42_mk1.php)
- **Vanguard (1946):** the first RN 15-in with remote power control (RPC) for training (Mk I/N RP12); elevation had no RPC. — [NavWeaps, 15"/42 Mk I](http://www.navweaps.com/Weapons/WNBR_15-42_mk1.php)
- **KGV (1939) 14-in Mk II/III mountings:** "Training was by one of two swashplate engines, 160 bhp in the quads and 70 bhp in the twin"; "Elevation was by hydraulic cylinder and piston connected to the rear of the cradle"; "the shell ring was driven by a 24 bhp hydraulic engine"; hydraulic Asbury breech. The rates are 2 deg/s train and 8 deg/s elevation. "RPC equipment was never fitted to any of these ships". — [NavWeaps, British 14"/45 Mark VII](http://www.navweaps.com/Weapons/WNBR_14-45_mk7.php)
- **Invincible:** "electrically driven BIX and BX turrets, two each built by Vickers and Armstrongs" (Roberts p. 83). They "proved to be a failure despite two lengthy refits in 1909 and 1911". At the October 1908 gunnery trials "the result was often a flash of blue flame which seemed to fill the turret" (Tupper, quoted in Roberts p. 85). "When the order was given to train the turret, elevate or run a gun in or out, it was only necessary to push a button" (Tupper). She was "converted to hydraulic power during her refit in early 1914 at a cost of £151,200". — [Wikipedia, HMS Invincible (1907)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Invincible_(1907)); [Wikipedia, Invincible-class battlecruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invincible-class_battlecruiser)
- Invincible's sisters had "four hydraulically powered BVIII twin-gun turrets". Lion (four twin hydraulically powered turrets), Tiger ("four twin hydraulically powered turrets", Campbell p. 35) and Renown ("three twin hydraulically powered gun turrets", Burt p. 297) were hydraulic too. — [Invincible class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invincible-class_battlecruiser); [Lion class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion-class_battlecruiser); [Tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Tiger_(1913)); [Renown class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renown-class_battlecruiser)
- **6-in turrets:** the committee quoted by Portsmouth Dockyard found electricity used for "the latest 6-inch turret designs" (date disputed, see section 1). — [Portsmouth Dockyard, "15. Electrics"](https://portsmouthdockyard.org.uk/chips15.html)
- **Prince of Wales (1941):** loss of power took out the after 5.25-in turrets' training and elevation and the pom-poms. "The aft group had no electric power at all". So the 5.25-in DP mountings depended on electric power. The report mentions no loss of the 14-in turrets' hydraulic power from the electrical failure. — [*Death of a Battleship* 2012](https://pacificwrecks.com/ship/hms/prince-of-wales/death-of-a-battleship-2012-update.pdf)

### Inferences
- [EST] The only RN heavy turrets before 1930 that depended on electric motors to train, elevate and run out were Invincible's four, from 1908 to 1914. They were gone before the battles of 1914-16 she fought. Every other dreadnought heavy turret ran off steam-driven hydraulic pumping engines on a ring main. An electrical trip could still take out lighting, turret fans, firing circuits and director transmission (section 3), but not the turret's motive power.
- [EST] Hood's four pumping engines for four turrets, and Nelson's one 750 hp steam engine per turret (existing notes), suggest one pumping engine per turret as the dreadnought norm.
- [EST] KGV's 160 bhp swashplate engines draw on the hydraulic main, which is consistent with the Nelson practice of steam pumping engines. Whether KGV's main pumps were steam- or electric-driven stays open. The PoW report's silence on 14-in power loss, while the electric 5.25-in mountings were lost, weakly suggests the 14-in hydraulics did not hang on the failed generators.

### Gaps
- No source found for the pumping engines' ihp, pressure (the ~1,000 psi in the brief is unverified), or accumulators, for any ship from Dreadnought to QE.
- Not resolved whether KGV's (1939) or the rebuilt QEs' hydraulic pumps were electric- or steam-driven.

## 3. What was electric on a WWI RN battleship

### Takeaway
By 1914-20 electricity drove lighting, ventilation fans, after capstans and boat hoists, coaling, ash and ammunition hoists, searchlights, telephones, fire-control transmission and some pumps. Main steering was by steam engines under telemotor control. Hood (1920) adds electric auxiliary steering, after capstan, boat hoists, fans, bilge and fire pumps. An electric trip would black out turret lighting and fans, the director and transmitting-station data, and electric ammunition hoists for the secondaries. On every heavy turret but Invincible's (to 1914) it would not stop the hydraulic training, elevation and ramming.

### Cited findings
- The committee's survey (Portsmouth Dockyard; date disputed) found electric power used for "lighting, fans, after capstans, the latest 6-inch turret designs, and ammunition supply, coal, ash and boat hoists". It recommended electric drive for all auxiliary machinery outside the engine room. — [Portsmouth Dockyard, "15. Electrics"](https://portsmouthdockyard.org.uk/chips15.html)
- **Dreadnought (1906):** the 100 V and 15 V DC systems powered "five lifts", "eight coaling winches, pumps, ventilation fans, lighting and telephone systems". — [Wikipedia, HMS Dreadnought (1906)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Dreadnought_(1906))
- **Hood (1920), steering:** two steering engines in the after engine room, worked by telemotor from the conning tower, lower conning tower and after engine room. The auxiliary steering was "Williams-Janney principle, driven by an electric motor" in the after steering compartment. No hand steering. — [ASNE Journal extract](https://www.hmshood.org.uk/reference/written/asnejournal32.htm)
- **Hood (1920), other electric gear:** engine-room supply fans (four 30-in, three 40-in) and exhaust fans (four 35-in, two 50-in), all electric; the after capstan for the 3-ton stern anchor, by Williams-Janney gear and an electric motor; boat hoists by electric motor with Williams-Janney variable-speed control, lifting 16 tons at 60 ft/min; electric bilge pumps and 100-ton submersible pumps; 50-ton electric fire-main pumps. Steam remained for the 75-ton fire and bilge pumps and the 1,000-ton turbo pumps in the boiler rooms. The main capstan engine's drive is not stated. — [ASNE Journal extract](https://www.hmshood.org.uk/reference/written/asnejournal32.htm)
- **Directors:** the Indefatigable class's director "electrically transmitted training and elevation angles to the gun turrets". — [Wikipedia, Indefatigable-class battlecruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indefatigable-class_battlecruiser)
- **Prince of Wales (1941), for comparison:** steering was electric by then. "Electric steering gear was knocked out" and a standby steam connection from X Engine Room was tried. Fans, lighting (including emergency lighting aft), radar, communications, pumps, the 5.25-in turrets and the pom-poms all lost power. — [*Death of a Battleship* 2012](https://pacificwrecks.com/ship/hms/prince-of-wales/death-of-a-battleship-2012-update.pdf)

### Inferences
- [EST] On an RN dreadnought of 1914-16, an electric trip would cause:
  - heavy turrets: they kept training and elevating on hydraulics (Invincible after 1914 included), but lost lighting, fans and the director's training and elevation pointers;
  - firing circuits: lost, unless a battery or percussion fallback was fitted (not sourced);
  - 6-in and 4-in secondaries: lost their electric ammunition hoists;
  - steering: kept going, since the main gear was steam under telemotor (Hood still had steam steering engines in 1920). Electric main steering is a 1930s change, as Prince of Wales shows.
- [EST] By the existing notes (Marlborough), a lower power board trip would knock out the electric hoists, fans and lighting forward. It would not stop the hydraulic turret machinery.

### Gaps
- No source found for whether WWI 12-in, 13.5-in and 15-in turrets' cordite and shell hoists, or the gun firing circuits, were electric or hydraulic. NavWeaps' 15-in page describes the turrets as hydraulic throughout but gives no hoist motors.
- No source found for Iron Duke's, QE's or Revenge's 6-in battery hoists, or for which ships had electric capstans before Hood.
- No contemporary 1906-1916 load list or Admiralty handbook was found online.
