## Case table: every case found in this pass (new ones and corrections to the earlier note)

### Takeaway
Ship-wide blackouts come from water reaching the switchboard or dynamo spaces (Lion 1915), from an underwater explosion cutting the ring main (Inflexible 1915), or from a ship's last stage as she sinks (Blücher). Shellfire on its own gives local outages: circuits cut in one compartment, lights out in one flat, switches knocked off by concussion and re-made by hand. Several heavily hit ships record no ship-wide loss: Derfflinger, Warspite, Princess Royal and Seydlitz (she lost a third of her supply to a torpedo but kept the rest).

### Inferences
- **[EST]** Counting heavy-shell hits, against an accounting in which only ship-wide outages count:
  - *Lion*: 16 heavy hits, total blackout from the 2-3 that flooded below the waterline.
  - *Derfflinger*: 21 heavy hits, no ship-wide loss in her gunnery officer's account.
  - *Warspite*: about 15 heavy hits, local only.
  - *Princess Royal*: about 9 heavy hits, local only.
  - *Seydlitz*: about 22 heavy hits and a torpedo, one third of supply lost, lighting tripped and restored by re-switching.
  - *Lützow*: more than 20 hits, lighting out about 45 min.
  - *Blücher*: 70-100 hits and torpedoes, total blackout at the end.

  On this sample a ship-wide blackout from a heavy shell above water is a rare event, under about 1 in 50 hits. Blackout tracks water reaching the switchboard or dynamo spaces, or an underwater explosion, not the hit count.
- **[EST]** Three severities show up again and again, which suggests modelling them separately:
  1. Concussion trips: lights and telegraphs out, switches open, fuses blown. Restored in minutes by re-switching (Seydlitz, Marlborough, Princess Royal's Argo tower).
  2. Local circuit severance: circuits in one compartment or flat cut, lasting the rest of the action (Warspite, Princess Royal's turret flat).
  3. Loss of a generating group or the main distribution: ship-wide or a fraction of supply, lasting hours or until port (Lion, Inflexible, Seydlitz's forward generators, Blücher).
- **[EST]** Ventilation is a weak point for the plant itself. Seydlitz's dynamo room was driven out by heat once its air leads were cut. Inflexible's engine rooms heated up when the fans died. A model could let a dynamo room's crew leave, and its output fall, when its ventilation is lost, rather than only when it is hit or flooded.
- **[EST]** Ships went into action with part of the lighting deliberately cut (Princess Royal's upper and main decks), and kept oil lamps or candles as secondary lighting (Inflexible, Lion). So "dark" did not mean helpless. Lion's galley still worked. Steam-driven essentials (engines, many pumps, steering in Audacious) went on after the electrics failed, until the water reached them.
- **[EST]** Fire-control transmission was lost more often by cut leads, gas or smoke in the transmitting station than by a loss of supply. Derfflinger's central was vacated for gas and control moved to the forward station. Princess Royal's fore distributing station was smoked out. The Borodinos' turret electrical connections failed while the turret machinery held.

## German electrical practice (and Japanese, briefly)

### Takeaway
By 1909-1914 German capital ships ran DC at 220-225 V from several turbo-generators. From the Derfflinger and König classes on they added diesel generators, and the plant was split into forward and after groups, so a single hit or torpedo cost a fraction of the supply (Seydlitz lost one third). No source on fuses against breakers, or on German action switching routines, was found. No Japanese material was found.

### Cited Findings
- *Blücher* (1909): "six turbo-generators that provided up to 1,000 kilowatts, rated at 225 volts". — [Wikipedia, SMS Blücher, citing Gröner p. 53](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMS_Bl%C3%BCcher)
- Derfflinger class (1914-17): "a pair of turbo-electric generators and a pair of diesel-electric generators that provided a total of 1,660 kilowatts at 220 volts". — [Wikipedia, Derfflinger class, citing Gröner p. 57](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derfflinger-class_battlecruiser)
- König class (1914): "four turbo generators and a pair of diesel generators", 2,040 kW at 225 V. — [Wikipedia, König class, citing Gröner p. 28 and Dodson p. 222](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6nig-class_battleship)
- *Seydlitz* had forward and after generators. Losing "both forward generators" to a torpedo cost one third of the supply, and the circuits were moved to the after generators. — [von Egidy](http://www.gwpda.org/naval/jut01.htm)
- German crews drilled working in the dark (blind-man's-bluff drills), and a Seydlitz petty officer re-made all the circuits from memory. — [von Egidy](http://www.gwpda.org/naval/jut01.htm)
- *Lützow*'s diesel dynamo room kept her lit while her bow flooded (earlier note). *Inflexible*'s diesel dynamo room existed by 1915 and was partly flooded by the mine. — [Salving of HMS Inflexible](https://library.imarest.org/record/10483/files/v5b14p01.pdf)
- For comparison, the Russian practice behind the Tsushima cases: French-built *Tsesarevich* had six steam-driven generators, 550 kW in all, with the 12-in and the six twin 6-in turrets "all electrically driven". — [naval-encyclopedia.com, Tsesarevich](https://naval-encyclopedia.com/ww1/russia/tsesarevich.php) (sources listed but not tied to claims)

### Inferences
- **[EST]** The German plant's layout made "lose a group" more likely than "lose everything" (Seydlitz's one third). Its voltage, 220-225 V against the RN's 220 V in newer ships (earlier note), gives no difference worth modelling.
- **[EST]** Diesel generators were in service in 1914-16 on both sides (König, Derfflinger, Lützow, Marlborough, Inflexible). They give a source independent of steam that survives losing boiler rooms, which matches Szent István keeping lights after her boiler rooms flooded (earlier note).
