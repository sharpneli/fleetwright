# D. Night identification (challenge and reply, recognition lights, holding fire, friendly fire) and night doctrine, written vs done
Status: draft    Updated: 2026-10-29    Request: [REQUESTS.md "Night fighting, 1890-1925 first"](../../REQUESTS.md); an appendix of [night-fighting.md](../night-fighting.md)

Strand D of the night research for Fleetwright (era 1890-1925 first; WWII as a later check).

Tags: **[SRC]** a source fetched in this session says it (quote or close paraphrase; source number in brackets); **[INFERRED]** my reading of the sources; **[EST]** my estimate, no source; **[UNCERTAIN]** a lead from memory or a weak or secondary source that I could not confirm.

**How this was made, and its limits.** About 45 fetches. The shared web-search budget ran out partway through, so the Russian (Makarov), Japanese (written doctrine), French, Austro-Hungarian, Italian and USN parts are thin (see "Not reached"). Primary text came from the archive.org full-text index and page OCR of:
- the 1920 *Battle of Jutland Official Despatches* [1];
- Corbett, *Naval Operations* vol. III [2];
- Jellicoe, *The Grand Fleet 1914-16* [3];
- Scheer, *Germany's High Sea Fleet* [4].

Two cautions on that method:
- The archive.org page numbers ("p.") are the full-text index's leaf numbers, not printed page numbers.
- The OCR quotes were relayed through a summarising fetch tool, so the wording may differ slightly from the printed text.

**German recognition lights as British reports described them at Jutland** [SRC 1]:
- "four red lights horizontal above four green horizontal", challenged three times; one report has 4 green then 4 red;
- "two red over one white light";
- "three vertical lights, the upper two being red and lower green";
- "two red lights over one green for a few seconds, then … searchlights";
- "a red light over a green just before the action commenced";
- "switching on and off several groups of Green and Red lamps".

Several patterns appear in one night. Either the signal changed by time and by squadron, or the observers saw different parts of it [INFERRED].

### Ranges at which identification was settled (Jutland night, British accounts)
- 150 yd: *Broke* vs *Westfalen*.
- ~200 yd: *Pelican*.
- ~400 yd / 2 cables: *Broke* at 01:15.
- ~500 yd: German torpedo boats vs 4th Flotilla.
- 600-1,000 yd: *Tipperary*.
- 800-1,500 yd: 2nd LCS.
- ~1,100 m: *Frankfurt*.
- ~1,500 m: *Black Prince*.
- 2,000 yd: *Castor*.

Median ≈ 600-800 yd (≈ 550-750 m) [INFERRED from the cases above]. The torpedo attacks that followed identification ran out to 3,000-5,000 yd (12th Flotilla) [SRC 1, p.34].

### How long a challenge took
- **British (1916):** a lamp challenge of two letters, answered by two letters [SRC 1, p.90: "answered by 2 letters"]. James (1944) recalls it as "colored lights" [SRC 14], which conflicts with the despatches. [UNCERTAIN] which is right for 1916; perhaps different systems for destroyers and big ships.
- **German:** lamp groups shown "for a few seconds", sometimes three times over [SRC 1].
- **Time to answer:** in every German answer reached, searchlight and fire came within seconds of the challenge, or before it was complete (*Broke*, *Westfalen*, *Ardent*, *Kolberg*, *Castor* after the partial reply) [SRC 1, 2, 4] [INFERRED: under ~10 s].
- No source gives a timed interval [no evidence].

### Rates (no source has any; inferred from the case list)
- **Jutland night, unknowns met by the British battle fleet and its rear** (*Thunderer*, *Agincourt*, *Revenge*, *Marlborough*, *Malaya*, *Valiant*, *Iron Duke*): every unknown large ship was in fact German. Fire was held in about 6 of the 7 cases (*Iron Duke* fired one gun) [INFERRED from cases 11 and 17-19, 25].
- **Light forces:** in at least 5 cases British destroyers or light cruisers first took Germans for friends (cases 9, 15, 16, 20, 21). Only one British-on-British firing case was found (none confirmed). The night's friend-on-friend damage at Jutland came from collisions (*Broke*/*Sparrowhawk*) [INFERRED].
- **Dogger Bank 1904:** 100% of the "torpedo boats" were trawlers or own cruisers. Fire was opened in 10-12 min of firing with no challenge [SRC 5].

## 2. Identification systems by navy

| Navy / year | System | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| RN 1914-16 | Challenge and reply by flashing lamp: two letters each. Changed at set times: *Lion* asked for "challenge and reply now in force", so there was a current pair by time period. Practice: challenge an unknown and "withhold fire until she had an opportunity of making the correct reply" (James). Jellicoe after Jutland: "Their system of recognition signals is excellent. Ours is practically nil." | [SRC 1, p.13, 90, 593]; [SRC 14] |
| RN after Jutland | Destroyer screens ordered "to open fire on every ship that approached, without challenging when in waters where the enemy was expected"; "heavy ships should not attempt to join the main body during the night". Searchlights became electrically worked from control positions; star shell introduced. | [SRC 14] |
| German 1915-16 | Coloured lamp groups (red, green, white) in vertical or horizontal rows, switched on and off, repeated. Answered by searchlight and fire when the other ship was judged enemy. At Jutland the Germans had part of the British challenge: the first two signs correct (Corbett). Secondary accounts: *Lion*'s lamp signal giving the challenge and reply was seen; *Seydlitz* used British "J" and was answered "according to some accounts". | [SRC 1, 2, 4]; [SRC 9, 10] (secondary) |
| Russian 1904 | No challenge in the Dogger Bank record. The admiral marked targets with his searchlight; ships swept with their own; the officer of the watch could fire on an "evident and imminent" torpedo attack. A ship identified herself by signalling her number (*Donskoi*); "cease firing" was by signal / blue light. | [SRC 5, 6] |
| IJN 1904-05 | Not reached. | — |
| USN 1900s-20s | Not reached. | — |

## 3. Night doctrine by navy and period, written vs done

| Navy, period | Topic | Written / ordered | Done | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **RN 1914-16** | Fleet night action | Avoid it. Jellicoe: "not my intention to seek such an action between the heavy ships". A night action "must be fought at very close range, the decision depending on the course of events in the first few minutes"; "very largely a matter of chance". | Grand Fleet steamed south through the night, no heavy-ship action | [SRC 3] |
| | Close order | Divisions in line ahead, "columns one mile apart" so "adjacent columns should not lose sight of each other … and … would not mistake our own ships for those of the enemy"; 17 kn | Done | [SRC 3] |
| | Destroyers | Stationed astern: in position to attack if the enemy turned south, and "clear of our own ships", so that "the danger of their attacking our battleships in error, or of our battleships firing on them" was minimal. "Our own destroyers … would certainly be taken for enemy destroyers and be fired on by our own ships." | Flotillas met the High Seas Fleet piecemeal astern. Torpedo ranges: 800 yd (*Garland*) up to 3,000 yd (12th Flotilla at 02:00) and 4,000-5,000 yd (*Maenad*). | [SRC 3]; [SRC 1, p.34, 46] |
| | Searchlights | Hand-worked, poor: "neither our search-lights nor their control arrangements were at this time of the best type"; no director for the secondary armament. James: "manipulated by hand to orders received through voice pipes". | Little used. Ships that kept them off were not seen (*Nottingham*, *Birmingham*). | [SRC 3, 14, 11] |
| | Star shell | None ("at that time unfamiliar to us") | None | [SRC 3] |
| | Challenge | Challenge, then withhold fire until a reply | Battleships often did not challenge, to avoid showing the fleet (*Agincourt*). Destroyers challenged at 2-10 cables and were answered with fire. | [SRC 14, 1] |
| | Hold fire | Implicit: don't show the battle fleet "unless obvious attack was intended" (*Thunderer*'s captain) | Widespread. Unknowns, including a wrong reply, were let through. | [SRC 10, 1] |
| **RN after Jutland, 1917-18** | | Director control of all guns; searchlights worked electrically from control positions; star shell; screens to fire without challenging where the enemy is expected; heavy ships not to rejoin at night | (No night fleet action to test it) | [SRC 14] |
| **German 1916** | Fleet at night | Main Fleet "in close formation" for Horns Reef by the shortest route; course S.S.E. ¾ E, 16 kn (order of 23:06); leaders of torpedo boats "to arrange night attacks for the flotillas" | Fleet pushed through the British rear. Flotillas II, V, VII and parts of VI and IX attacked but "never sighted the Main Fleet". | [SRC 4, p.181-182] |
| | Searchlights | Lights trained before being switched on, then light and fire at once. Jellicoe: "method of controlling them and bringing guns and searchlights rapidly on to any vessel sighted was excellent". British despatch: "Searchlights were already trained on the ship before being switched on." Scheer: "Very little use was made of the searchlights"; the attacking boats' fire "was aimed chiefly at these illuminated targets". | Light-and-fire at 150-2,000 yd in every case found. Searchlights went out when the ship was hit or turned away. | [SRC 3, 1 p.281, 4 p.182] |
| | Star shell | — | Used "in locating our destroyers without revealing their own positions" (Jellicoe), i.e. to search as well as to fire | [SRC 3] |
| | Recognition | Coloured-lamp challenge | Shown first, then lit and fired. Sometimes torpedoes fired dark without lights or guns (*Frankfurt*, *Pillau*). | [SRC 1, 8] |
| | Turn away | Evading torpedoes "by suitable manoeuvring … demanded the most careful observation" | Battleships turned away from destroyer attacks. During one such turn *Posen* rammed *Elbing*; the turn is [INFERRED], the ramming [SRC 9]. | [SRC 4, 9] |
| | Pre-war | — | Jellicoe: "German organisation for night action was of a remarkably high standard". [UNCERTAIN] Boston Sea Rovers: the fleet "extensively trained" in its own searchlight use. | [SRC 3]; [SRC 15] |
| **Russian 1904-05** | Passage orders (Rozhestvensky) | "No vessel of any sort must be allowed to get in among the fleet"; the officer of the watch "authorized to open fire" on an evident and imminent torpedo attack; the admiral marks targets with his searchlight | Dogger Bank: 10-12 min of fire on trawlers and on *Aurora* | [SRC 5, 6] |
| | Port Arthur (Feb 1904) | Patrol destroyers ordered "not to initiate combat" | Japanese surprise; afterwards searchlights and gunfire kept attackers off | [SRC 7] |
| | Tsushima night (27/28 May 1905) | — | Ships "revealed their positions … by switching on their searchlights … to spot the attackers". Lost *Navarin*, *Sissoi Veliky*, *Admiral Nakhimov*, *Vladimir Monomakh*. [UNCERTAIN] Nebogatov's ships kept dark and were not torpedoed. | [SRC 16] |
| | Makarov 1897 | Not reached | — | — |

## 4. Against the game's estimates

| Game estimate | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Challenge by signal lamp ~3·10⁴ cd, 10° wide | **No evidence** on intensity or beam | The British challenge was two lamp letters; the German one, coloured lamp groups (red/green/white), several lamps at once, "for a few seconds" [SRC 1]. A German-style challenge should be several coloured lamps, visible all round rather than a 10° beam [INFERRED]. |
| Held 8 s | **Plausible** [EST] | German groups "for a few seconds", repeated up to three times [SRC 1]. Two Morse letters at signalling speed take a few seconds [EST]. |
| Reply awaited 20 s; unanswered → foe | **Revise** | The awaiting side rarely got 20 s. Germans answered a British challenge with searchlight and fire within seconds (*Broke* ×2, *Ardent*, *Tipperary*, *Kolberg*) [SRC 1, 4]. On the other side, "unanswered or wrong → foe" did *not* make the British battle line fire: *Revenge* got "2 letters, though they were not the correct ones" and held fire; only *Iron Duke* fired a gun [SRC 1]. Suggestion [EST]: keep the 20 s timeout, but (a) the challenge is a light the other ship sees and can fire at, and (b) whether "foe" leads to firing is the doctrine's hold-fire rule, not automatic. |
| Answered → friend for 10 min | **No evidence** for a duration. A partly right reply (*Castor*'s 2 of 4 signs) made consorts hold fire even while *Castor* was being shot [SRC 2, 1]. | — |
| One challenge at a time | **No evidence** | — |
| Default: challenge unknowns within 2,500 m, dark beyond | **Roughly confirmed, the observed range is shorter** | Challenges and identification at Jutland at 150-2,000 yd (140-1,830 m), median ~600-800 yd [INFERRED]. Battleships often did not challenge at all, to stay hidden (*Agincourt*) [SRC 1]. Suggest 2,000 m for destroyers and cruisers and "don't challenge" for a battle line hiding [EST]. |
| Searchlights "to fire" | **Confirmed for the Germans 1916** | Trained dark, then switched on with fire [SRC 1, 3]. RN 1916 in practice "never" (poor lights) [SRC 3, 14]. Russians 1904-05 "to search", and were punished at Tsushima [SRC 16]. |
| Star shell "to fire" | **Revise for the Germans** | Used "in locating our destroyers without revealing their own positions" [SRC 3], i.e. *to search* as well. RN: none until after Jutland [SRC 3, 14]. |
| When lit, turn away (douse, stern to the light, hold 120 s) | **Direction confirmed, timing no evidence** | British destroyers lit, fired a torpedo and turned away (*Castor*, *Magic*, *Marne* "turned to port") [SRC 1, 2]. German battleships turned away from attacks (*Posen*/*Elbing*) [SRC 9] [INFERRED]. Ships kept dark were not engaged (*Nottingham*, *Birmingham*) [SRC 11]. Exchanges lasted ~5 min (*Castor*, secondary) to ~15 min (*Southampton*'s own report) [SRC 12]. No figure for 120 s. |
| Lines close to half their day spacing at night | **Direction confirmed, ratio no evidence** | Jellicoe closed columns to one mile apart to keep sight and avoid mistaking his own ships [SRC 3]. Scheer: "close formation" [SRC 4]. The day spacing to compare with was not reached. |
| Bold captain fires on an unknown if no friend is expected within 5 km × (1 − aggression) | **Supported in kind** | Russian 1904 standing orders let the officer of the watch fire on an apparent torpedo attack, which led to friendly fire on *Aurora* [SRC 5]. RN after Jutland: screens fire on "every ship that approached, without challenging when in waters where the enemy was expected" [SRC 14]. A cautious captain's rule from the record: *Thunderer*, "unless obvious attack was intended" [SRC 10]. 5 km is [EST]: the cases were all within ~2 km. |
| Share of the enemy's challenge a side can answer: 0 by default | **Revise for Jutland** | Germans made "the first two signals … correct for the British challenge of the day" (Corbett) [SRC 2]. *Lion*'s lamp request for the challenge and reply was seen (secondary) [SRC 9, 10]. *Revenge* got 2 wrong letters [SRC 1]. Suggest for a Jutland scenario: the IV Scouting Group and the battlecruisers partly correct from ~22:00 [EST: 0.3-0.5, or a "partial reply" state that causes doubt rather than acceptance]. No evidence that the British could answer German lights; Wikipedia says they "could not duplicate" them [SRC 8]. |
