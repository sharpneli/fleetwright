# E1. Night actions and night torpedo attacks, 1890-1905, as numbers
Status: draft    Updated: 2026-10-29    Request: [REQUESTS.md "Night fighting, 1890-1925 first"](../../REQUESTS.md); an appendix of [night-fighting.md](../night-fighting.md)

Strand E1 of the night research for the game's night bench. Every claim carries a tag:

- **[SRC]**: stated by the source cited (number in brackets, list at the end).
- **[INFERRED]**: worked out from sourced facts. All moon and twilight figures are this kind: computed with PyEphem (script: [e1_moon.py](e1_moon.py)) for the place and night given.
- **[EST]**: my estimate, with the reasoning shown.
- **[UNCERTAIN]**: the sources conflict, or I read the source only through OCR snippets, or a paraphrase may have garbled it.

**How the sources were read.** archive.org's "search inside" API returned OCR snippets with page numbers (scan leaf numbers, not printed pages), and a small model paraphrased them. Quoted figures came through intact. Some attributions inside a passage, such as which boat fired which torpedo, are marked [UNCERTAIN] wherever the paraphrase looked ambiguous. The full texts could not be fetched past about 80k characters. The session's web-search budget ran out part-way, so no further searches could be made after that point. The later work used known URLs and archive.org's API only.

**Time zones.** Japanese times are presumably JST (UTC+9; Japan adopted it in 1888). Russian times at Port Arthur are local, about UTC+8:05, so about 55 minutes behind JST. The moon tables below are in JST for the Far East and in local mean time elsewhere.

## 1. Chile: *Blanco Encalada* sunk at Caldera Bay, 23 April 1891

| Date / time | Light | Forces | First sighting | Lights | Ranges | Torpedoes / hits | Losses | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 23 Apr 1891. Entry about 03:30, sighted about 04:00 [SRC 1] | Moon 99% full (full moon 24 Apr). Altitude 27° at 03:30 and 21° at 04:00, in the west. "Though there was a moon, it was, from time to time, hidden by the clouds" [SRC 1]; [INFERRED moon figures] | 2 torpedo gunboats, *Almirante Condell* and *Almirante Lynch* (5 tubes each, about 21 kn), with the steamer *Imperial* following. Against them, the ironclad *Blanco Encalada* at anchor [SRC 1,2] | *Blanco*'s watch saw them at about 2,000 yd, about 04:00 [SRC 1] | Attackers carried no lights at night [SRC 1]. *Blanco* used no searchlight: "out of order or unnecessary in the daylight" [SRC 1]. No nets, launches not on patrol [SRC 1] | *Blanco* opened fire when the leader was 500 yd off [SRC 1]. *Condell* fired at about 100 yd. *Lynch* fired at about 50 yd and 100 yd [SRC 1,2] | 5 fired, 2 hits. One hit was a dud. *Lynch*'s hit amidships sank her [SRC 1,2,3]. Hervey: *Lynch*'s 1st sank, 2nd ran wide, 3rd hit [SRC 1] [UNCERTAIN: count per boat] | *Blanco* sunk in about 2 min; 182 of 288 dead (11 officers and 171 men), about 40 of them said killed by the boats' machine guns [SRC 1,2]. *Lynch* slightly damaged: steam pipe, aft compartment flooded [SRC 2] | 7 min first to last torpedo [SRC 1]; WP: 9 min [SRC 2] |

**Mechanism.**

1. The boats entered dark on a cloudy, moonlit night. [SRC]
2. They were seen at about 2,000 yd. The crew was slow to quarters, and the first gun fired at 500 yd. At half speed the boats needed about 5 minutes to close 1,500 yd. [SRC 1]
3. *Condell* drew the ship's fire. *Lynch* came in "unnoticed from the opposite side" to 50-100 yd. [SRC 2]
4. Wilson's verdict: the ship lay "without nets, launches, or a search-light", with a poorly trained crew, and had two days' warning. [SRC 1]

**Notes.**

- Wilson calls the 03:30 entry "dawn" [SRC 1]. The computed sun is 38° below the horizon at 03:30 local mean time, and nautical dawn was about 05:30. The light was a near-full moon through cloud, not dawn [INFERRED]. Either the source's times are in another reckoning, or "dawn" is loose [UNCERTAIN].
- The recovered torpedo was a Whitehead Mark IV set for 600 yd [SRC 1].

The boats waited for the moonset on a cloudy, "very dark" night [SRC 1]; [INFERRED moonset] | 4 torpedo craft: *Gustavo Sampaio* (torpedo gunboat, 3 tubes) and *Affonso Pedro*, *Pedro Ivo*, *Silvado* (130 t, 26 kn, 24 men, 3 tubes) [SRC 1]. The first attempt, on the night of 14-15 Apr, was foiled when the insurgents burned Bengal lights [SRC 1]. The 2nd was fired passing at about 400 ft (130 yd); a 3-second tube delay put the hit forward [SRC 1]. *Sampaio* lay stopped 90 s at 150 yd under fire. *Affonso Pedro* passed within 200 yd untouched [SRC 1] | Wilson: *Sampaio* 2 fired, 1 hit; another boat fired 2 "of uncertain effect" [SRC 1]. WP: "two torpedoes connected" [SRC 4] [UNCERTAIN: 1 or 2 hits] | *Aquidabã* sank in shallow water, later raised [SRC 1,4]. *Sampaio* took 38 one-inch Nordenfelt hits, 1 man slightly wounded. *Silvado* took 1 hit [SRC 1] | Minutes [EST] |

**Mechanism.**

1. The attackers waited for the moon to set.
2. A decoy steamer drew the watch's attention.
3. The boats attacked in the dark from close range.
4. The defender lit up only after the hit. Her light weapons (1-inch Nordenfelts) could not stop a boat even at 150 yd. [SRC 1]
5. Wilson's lessons: *Aquidabã* "betrayed her position"; machine guns are useless against torpedo craft; a 12-pdr is the right weight [SRC 1].

## 3. Sino-Japanese War: Weihaiwei, 4-5 and 5-6 February 1895

The Japanese had 16 boats in 3 divisions:

- I Division: *Kotaka*, Nos. 23, 7, 11, 12, 13.
- II Division: Nos. 21, 8, 9, 14, 18, 19.
- III Division: Nos. 22, 5, 6, 10.

[SRC 1]

The eastern boom was about 3,300 yd of steel hawsers on floats, with a central opening 300 ft wide [SRC 1]. An earlier attack, on the night of 2 Feb, failed when the Chinese spotted the boats [SRC 1].

| Night | Light | Forces | First sighting | Lights | Ranges | Torpedoes / hits | Losses | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **4-5 Feb**. Steered for the boom about 03:00, "when the moon went down". Attack opened 04:00 [SRC 1]. ja.WP: 03:20 [SRC 7] | Moon 67% waxing, setting about 03:40 JST (about 02:50 local mean time) [INFERRED]. "Eighteen degrees of frost", about −10 °C: spray froze, tubes iced [SRC 1] | II and III Divisions, 10 boats, from the east. I Division feinted at the west entrance [SRC 1] | Not stated. The Chinese opened heavy fire (Gatling, rifle, 1-pdr Hotchkiss) as the boats closed [SRC 1] | No mention of searchlights in Wilson's account [SRC 1] | No. 10 hit *Ting Yuen* (*Dingyuan*) from about 300 yd; her bow tube was iced, so she fired the broadside tube. No. 5 "fired too early" and missed [SRC 1] | At least 7 fired (No. 5 ≥1, No. 22: 3, No. 10: 1, No. 6: 1, which broke in two, No. 9: 1) [INFERRED count]. **1 hit**, on *Dingyuan*, who was grounded and later scuttled [SRC 1,6] | Nos. 8 and 21 grounded on the way in and took no part. No. 22 lost on the rocks, 2 of her men drowned of cold. No. 9 boilers burst by a shell, 2 killed and 4 scalded to death, sank under tow. No. 6: 46 rifle hits and 1 Hotchkiss shell. Nos. 8 and 18: rudders or screws damaged on rocks or the boom [SRC 1] | About an hour [EST] |
| **5-6 Feb** | Moon 77%, setting about 04:50 JST [INFERRED]. Attack time not found [open] | I Division did "the main work" [SRC 1] | Not stated | Not stated | Not stated | Wilson: "seven torpedoes were fired by the *Kotaka* and Numbers 11 and 23" [SRC 1] [UNCERTAIN paraphrase]. *Lai Yuen* capsized, *Wei Yuen* sunk. Wilson also names *Ching Yuen* hit [SRC 1]; other accounts give a training ship and an auxiliary sunk [SRC 6] [UNCERTAIN] | Wilson's whole-operation total: "twelve men killed and two torpedo-boats sunk" [SRC 1]. No boat was sunk by Chinese gunfire [SRC 1] | n/a |

**Mechanism.**

1. The boats attacked an anchored fleet after moonset, in bitter cold.
2. They steered on the target's masts.
3. Rocks and the boom cost more boats than the Chinese guns did (4 put out on rocks or the boom, 1 by gunfire).
4. Ice jammed the tubes.

Wilson (1897): the boats did more than the Yalu. At least 14,000 t of ships were "injured". The Chinese lacked 12-pdr to 6-inch quick-firers [SRC 1].

## 4. Spanish-American War: Santiago de Cuba, June-July 1898

| Item | Data |
|---|---|
| US night blockade | At nightfall two armoured ships stood about **1,000 m** from the harbour mouth. One held its two searchlights on the whole entrance channel. The other lay in the dark on the flank, its battery trained to enfilade the exit. The watch was relieved **every 2 hours** [SRC 35, Concas, Cervera's flag captain]. By day the semicircle lay about 6 mi from the Morro and closed "discreetly" at night [SRC 34]. Mahan: a night exit meant about 1 mile of narrow channel "with the beam of an electric light shining straight in the eyes" [SRC 36] |
| Cervera's reasons | On 11 June Cervera signalled that leaving by night was impossible while the shore guns could not drive off the ships "whose electric searchlights constantly lit the harbour mouth" [SRC 35]. The forts did not fire at the lights: there were only two modern 16 cm guns, with about 100 rounds each [SRC 35]. The sortie was set for 09:00 on 3 July; waiting for night "would only make the escape even more treacherous" [SRC 34] |
| Mahan's worry | The dark of the moon (new on 18 June) [SRC 36]. Computed new moon: 19 Jun 1898 UTC [INFERRED] |
| *Merrimac*, 3 June 1898 | Planned "by moonlight just before moonset". Hobson gave the moonset as about 03:30 [SRC 32]; computed 04:04 local mean time, moon 97% full [INFERRED]. She went in with the moon "about an hour and a half high", keeping east of its reflection, at about 4 kn [SRC 32]. **First detection: by a dark picket boat at the water's edge, whose 2 shots came at "very short range", within about a ship's length** [SRC 32]. The steering gear was shot away; only 3 of 10 charges fired; she sank clear of the fairway [SRC 33]. No searchlight is named in her detection in what I read [open]. Crew of 8 all survived and were captured [SRC 33] |
| Spanish torpedo-boat destroyers at night | *Plutón* and *Furor* made no night attack in what I read. They were sunk in the day sortie of 3 July [SRC 34] [INFERRED: no night attack recorded] |

**Mechanism.** A searchlight held on a narrow exit, with a dark ship on the flank and frequent reliefs, made a night sortie unthinkable to the defender. The searchlight ships were never fired on, for want of guns and shells. The only night penetration (*Merrimac*) was found at a ship's length by a dark picket boat, not by a light.

## 5. Russo-Japanese War

### 5a. Port Arthur, night of 8-9 February 1904

| Date / time | Light | Forces | First sighting | Lights | Ranges | Torpedoes / hits | Losses | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| First 4 destroyers at about 00:28 on 9 Feb "without being observed"; last attack about 02:00 [SRC 9] (time zone not given; JST likely [UNCERTAIN]) | Moon 48% waning, **below the horizon until about 01:56 JST** [INFERRED]: the first attacks were moonless | 10 destroyers of the 1st-3rd Divisions for Port Arthur (4th and 5th sent to Dalny) [SRC 9,10]. Two collided after meeting a Russian patrol destroyer about 20:50 [SRC 9,10]. First wave: *Akatsuki*, *Asashio*, *Kasumi*, *Shirakumo*, *Ikazuchi* [SRC 13,14] | The attackers were not identified until torpedoes exploded: "boats showing lights were naturally taken for our own", one said to have answered "*Steregushchi*" [SRC 18, p.66] | Russians at anchor in the outer roads "showing their usual lights", no nets [SRC 18, p.31]. ***Retvizan* was on searchlight duty and "attracted many torpedoes"** [SRC 11]. Searchlights and gunfire by about 02:00 "made accurate and close range torpedo attacks impossible" [SRC 9] | First wave "closed to about 650 yd (590 m) and fired nine torpedoes" [SRC 13,14]. ja.WP's "about 10,000 m" is surely an error [SRC 10] [UNCERTAIN, discard] | **16 fired, 3 hits** [SRC 9]. Other figures circulate (about 18-20) [UNCERTAIN]. Hits: *Retvizan* (port bow, 220 sq ft hole, 2,200 t of water, 11° list cut to 5°, grounded) [SRC 11]; *Tsesarevich* (aft, 18° list) [SRC 12,18]; *Pallada* (by *Kasumi*) [SRC 14] | Japanese: no losses, slight damage [SRC 38] | About 1.5 h (00:28 to about 02:00) [SRC 9] |

**Mechanism.**

1. The attack came by surprise on a moonless night. The anchored ships were lit as in peacetime.
2. The attackers showing lights were taken for friendly destroyers.
3. The ship burning a searchlight (*Retvizan*) drew torpedoes.
4. After the alarm, searchlights and quick-fire kept later waves from closing, and they scored nothing [SRC 9; INFERRED from "of sixteen only three hit", all to the first attacks].

### 5d. Tsushima, night of 27-28 May 1905

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| From about 20:00, 27 May. All divisions but the 4th done by midnight. 4th Destroyer Division about 02:00-02:30 on 28 May [SRC 22,23,19] | Sunset 19:23, nautical dusk 20:27 JST. **Moon (about 36% waning) below the horizon until about 01:51**, so the main attacks were moonless [INFERRED]. Full moon was 18 May (Semenov, 16 May: "full moon two days away") [SRC 18, p.491]. High waves on the day [SRC 23]. Japanese: TBs **34 and 35 sunk by gunfire, 69 by collision with *Akatsuki*** [SRC 23]; Tōgō's report has all 3 to shellfire [SRC 19]. Damaged: *Harusame*, *Akatsuki*, *Ikazuchi*, *Yūgiri*, TBs *Sagi*, 68, 33 [SRC 19]; *Yūgiri* and *Harusame* in collision [SRC 23] | About 3 h main phase (20:00-23:00) [SRC 22] plus the 4th Division about 02:00 |

**Ship by ship.**

- ***Vladimir Monomakh***: claimed a torpedo boat sunk at 20:25. Hit by 1 torpedo about 20:40, which ruptured her near No. 2 coal bunker. Her captain had taken an attacker for a Russian destroyer [SRC 24]. EN WP *Battle of Tsushima* instead says she collided with a Japanese destroyer [SRC 22] [UNCERTAIN].
- ***Sissoi Veliky***: sighted destroyers fanning out at 19:30. Beat off attacks at 19:45 and 22:30 at under 600 yd. The third attack, about 45 min later (about 23:15 [INFERRED]), hit her stern: rudder and screws [SRC 25]. Credited there to Suzuki's 4th Division [SRC 25]; ja.WP presumes the 4th Division's mines at about 02:30 [SRC 23] [UNCERTAIN]. Bow under by 03:15; sank 10:05 next morning; 47 dead [SRC 25].
- ***Admiral Nakhimov***: last in the line, "visible because she was turning on searchlights". Hit at the bow about 21:30-22:00 [SRC 26]. Novikov: the destroyer that fired it was sunk by an 8-inch shell [SRC 20, a Russian claim]. Sank about 10:00 on 28 May; 18 lost [SRC 26].
- ***Navarin***: holed aft by day and stopped for repairs about 21:00, when torpedo boats attacked and "may have made one or two torpedo hits" [SRC 27].
  - Novikov's survivor's account: destroyers in two groups, one on each bow, "held her with searchlights" while a third crept up astern and fired before anyone saw it. A second hit amidships killed the dynamos [SRC 20, pp.386-387].
  - About 02:00 the 4th Division laid 6 strings of 4 linked mines ahead of her; 2 struck [SRC 27]. Tōgō's report instead says "hit by two torpedoes on each side", sunk "within a few minutes", 27 mi ENE of Karasaki [SRC 19, p.135] [UNCERTAIN: torpedoes or mines].
  - Her black-powder guns' smoke hid the destroyers from her own gunners [SRC 20, p.384].
- **Nebogatov's group** (*Nikolai I*, *Oryol*, *Apraksin*, *Senyavin*; *Ushakov* lagging): kept dark and was not torpedoed [SRC 20, 21; INFERRED from the list of the torpedoed].
- **Cruisers** *Oleg*, *Aurora*, *Zhemchug*: steamed without lights in "pitch darkness". The flagship used searchlights early on, against about 17 torpedoes fired at it, none hitting [SRC 21, pp.238,267].

**Checking the bench's line, "Russian ships that lit their searchlights were found and torpedoed".** It is **supported** for *Nakhimov* [SRC 20,26] and for the rear ships generally [SRC 21,22,23]. It is **partly supported** for *Navarin*: she was found because she stopped and fell out of line, and her attackers lit *her* [SRC 20,27]. For *Monomakh* and *Sissoi* I found no searchlight statement either way [open]. The dark group (Nebogatov's) was spared [SRC 20,21]. Hits per torpedo: about 4-6 hits from 54 torpedoes plus 32 linked mines, so **about 1 hit per 9-13 torpedoes** [EST].

### 5e. Dogger Bank incident, night of 21-22 October 1904

| Item | Data |
|---|---|
| Light | Moon 95% (full on 24 Oct), **about 23° high at 00:55** [INFERRED]. Fog patches mentioned [SRC 30]. So a bright moon in patchy haze |
| Forces | Rozhestvensky's battleship division (*Suvorov*, *Alexander III*, *Borodino*, *Oryol*) among the Hull "Gamecock" trawler fleet. The cruisers *Aurora* and *Dmitri Donskoi* nearby. The repair ship *Kamchatka* separately [SRC 30,31] |
| First sighting | Rozhestvensky saw unlit boats at 00:55 and ordered searchlights on [SRC 30]. The fleet came down on the trawlers "shortly before midnight" in two sections; the first passed after "shining searchlights on the trawlers" [SRC 31] |
| Ranges | A trawler in the searchlight glare "about five cable-lengths away" [SRC 20, p.65] |
| Fire | About 10 min until the cease-fire signal [SRC 30]; the Times said 20 min [SRC 30]. *Oryol* reportedly fired over 500 shells with no hits [SRC 30]. *Kamchatka*, earlier and elsewhere, claimed 300 shells against "three Japanese warships" [SRC 30; SRC 20, p.79] |
| Hits | Trawler *Crane* sunk, 4 others damaged; 2 fishermen killed (a third died later), 6 wounded [SRC 30,31]. *Aurora* hit by her own battleships: the chaplain and a sailor killed [SRC 30]. Shell count not found [open] |
| Confusion | Searchlights from *Aurora* and *Donskoi* on the port beam were taken for enemies, and the battleships "fired wildly at them". Russian challenge lamps (Tabulevich system) flashed above the searchlights [SRC 18, pp.308-309]. Gunners fired at "empty water lit by searchlight beams"; men reloaded unfired guns [SRC 20, p.63]. Cease-fire: all searchlights out except one pointed at the sky, the prearranged signal [SRC 18, p.309; SRC 20, p.65] |
| Findings | The International Commission found no torpedo boats on the Dogger Bank that night [SRC 31] |

**Mechanism.** Nerves, searchlights and close order together. A ship's own light picks out a neutral, and the glare blinds the gunners. The beams of the squadron's own cruisers read as enemy. Even under a bright moon, recognition at 5 cables failed.

Context from before 1890 (Wilson [SRC 1]):

- Foochow, 1884: a Chinese night torpedo attack fled when French searchlights exposed it.
- Sheipoo, 14-15 Feb 1885: a new moon and "absolutely dark" night. French spar-torpedo launches struck at about 200 yd. Of the two Chinese ships found sunk, one was probably sunk by Chinese fire on their own ship in the confusion.

## Patterns

1. **Attackers chose the dark.** Caldera's moon was hidden by cloud. At Santa Catarina and Weihaiwei (I) the boats attacked just after moonset. Port Arthur's first attack and Tsushima's main attacks came before moonrise. The Yellow Sea night had a new moon. [SRC + INFERRED from the computed moon]

2. **The defence lit up late, or not at all, at the anchorages.**
   - *Blanco* used no searchlight; *Aquidabã* lit only after the hit.
   - Port Arthur on 8 Feb was lit "as usual", but no one challenged boats that showed lights.

   In every pre-1904 anchorage case **the attacker struck first and the light came after the hit**. [SRC]

3. **A defence that lit up early and steadily held the attackers far off.**
   - Port Arthur, 23-24 Jun 1904: the fortress "wall of light" plus fire at 5-6 mi kept destroyers beyond 3 mi; their torpedoes were wasted.
   - Port Arthur, 9 Feb 1904: after about 02:00, searchlights and fire "made close range attacks impossible".
   - Santiago: a fixed searchlight on the exit.

   [SRC] **Fixed or shore lights with guns behind them protect; a ship's own light at sea marks the ship.** [INFERRED]

4. **At sea, the ship that lit up was the one found.**
   - *Retvizan* on searchlight duty "attracted many torpedoes".
   - *Nakhimov*'s lights drew destroyers "like moths".
   - Tsushima's rear battleships drew fire when they lit up; the leaders, dark, did not.
   - *Diana* stayed dark through the Yellow Sea night untouched.

   [SRC] **Supports the bench's rule, with the caveat that falling out of line (*Navarin*), being damaged, or being last in line also made a ship a target.** [INFERRED]

5. **Searchlight reach against small craft:** about 1.5-2 mi for a ship's own lights, weather permitting [SRC 18 footnote]. Cross Hill's beam "could not quite reach" vessels about 2 mi off [SRC 18]. Recognition at 5 cables failed at Dogger Bank even under a 95% moon [SRC/INFERRED].

6. **First detection without lights is short:** 2,000 yd at Caldera, under a clouded full moon. At Santiago a dark picket boat found the *Merrimac* at about one ship's length. At Port Arthur the boats were not known as enemies until the torpedoes exploded. Ships passed within 2-3 cables unseen at Tsushima [SRC 20, p.414]. A Russian destroyer cruised all night with a Japanese flotilla unrecognised (*Strashny*). [SRC]

7. **Launch ranges:**
   - 50-100 yd (1891); 130-150 yd (1894); about 300 yd (1895).
   - About 650 yd (1904, first wave); under 600 yd and down to 100 yd (Tsushima).

   Attackers who were kept at bay fired from **3 miles** believing they were at a few cables [SRC 18]. **Night range estimates by attackers were badly short.** [SRC]

8. **Hits per torpedo** [EST from the counts above]:
   - About 1 in 2.5 at Caldera.
   - About 1 in 7 at Weihaiwei (I).
   - About 1 in 5 at Port Arthur on 8 Feb.
   - About 1 in 9-13 at Tsushima's night.
   - 0 in 17 against moving cruisers (*Oleg*/*Aurora*); 0 for the Port Arthur roads on 23 Jun and the Yellow Sea night.

   **Ships at anchor were hit far more often than ships under way.** [INFERRED]

9. **Attackers' losses came mostly from the sea and from each other:**
   - Weihaiwei: 4 boats lost or put out on rocks or the boom against 1 to gunfire.
   - Collisions: Port Arthur 8 Feb (2 destroyers), Tsushima (TB 69 with *Akatsuki*, *Yūgiri* with *Harusame*), and *Strashny*'s fate.
   - Gunfire sank 2 of 3 at Tsushima; it sank none at Caldera, Santa Catarina or Port Arthur on 8 Feb.

   Light guns (1-inch, 1-pdr, rifles) hit boats often and seldom stopped them: *Sampaio* took 38 hits, No. 6 took 47. [SRC]

10. **Cold and sea degraded the attack:** tubes iced at Weihaiwei, and the swell on the Yellow Sea night [SRC]. **Friendly fire and false alarms:** Sheipoo, Dogger Bank, *Minerva* in 1900, the RN destroyer in 1900 [SRC].

11. **Self-blinding:** smoke from black-powder guns hid the destroyers (*Navarin*) [SRC 20]. Bursting shells were "brighter than the searchlights" [SRC 18, p.190]. Searchlights made only a "luminous mist" in haze [SRC 18, p.463].
