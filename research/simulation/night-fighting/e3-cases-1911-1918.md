# E3: Night actions 1911-1918 other than Jutland, as numbers
Status: draft    Updated: 2026-10-29    Request: [REQUESTS.md "Night fighting, 1890-1925 first"](../../REQUESTS.md); an appendix of [night-fighting.md](../night-fighting.md)

Strand E3 for the night model (darkness, searchlights, star shell, challenges, holding fire on unknowns, flotillas attacking from the dark side, ships turning away when lit).

**Tags.** [SRC] means a source says it, with the source number in brackets. [INFERRED] means worked from sourced facts; every moon figure is computed with PyEphem 4.2.1 from the sourced date, time and place (script: [e3_moon.py](e3_moon.py) beside this file). [EST] is my estimate. [UNCERTAIN] means the sources conflict or the point is unverified.

**Method note.** The official histories were read through archive.org's full-text search, one search term at a time. Each match came back paraphrased, with page numbers that are scan-page indices. So the numbers below are as the source gives them, but the wording is not verbatim. Some times in those paraphrases came out garbled ("2:45 p.m." for 02:45, "6.58" for a pre-dawn time). Where that happened it is marked.

**Coverage.** I ran out of WebSearch budget partway through (the shared limit across agents). The remaining cases were reached by fetching known pages directly, so the Baltic raid of November 1916, the Black Sea, the Italian 1912 Dardanelles raid and 21 March 1918 are thin. See "Not reached / open".

**Time zones used for the moon.** British times are taken as GMT, except 20-21 Apr 1917, which was computed both ways because British Summer Time began 8 Apr 1917. German and Austro-Hungarian times are CET in 1915 and CEST in summer 1916-18. Italian 1917-18 summer times are UTC+2. Ottoman, Bulgarian and Greek 1912 times are taken as about UTC+2. Coronel ship's time is about UTC-4h40m (fitted to the sourced 18:50 sunset).

## 1. The Dover Strait

### 1a. 26-27 October 1916 (the German 3rd and 9th Flotillas' raid)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Date and time | 26 Oct about 21:20 (first German sighting of British destroyers) to about 01:00 on 27 Oct [SRC 5] |
| Light | New moon at 20:37 UTC on 26 Oct; moon below the horizon (alt -52° at 22:00) [INFERRED]. Pitch dark: the Vice-Admiral waited ashore "in pitch darkness" [SRC 5] |
| Forces | German: 3rd and 9th Flotillas from Zeebrugge (Michelsen), in half-flotillas (5th, 6th, 17th, 18th), 23 boats [SRC 5, 3]. British: Flirt on the barrage; six Tribals at Dover (Viking, Mohawk, Tartar, Nubian, Cossack, Amazon); Laforey's and Lawford's divisions; 4 drifter divisions on the barrage; 130 net drifters in the command [SRC 5] |
| First sightings | 21:20: the German 18th Half Flotilla sees four British destroyers passing on the opposite course; it is not seen [SRC 5]. 21:35: Flirt's officer of the watch sees "several destroyers" on the port beam about 3 miles from the light buoy (the 3 miles is the buoy's distance, not the destroyers') [SRC 5] |
| Challenges | Flirt challenged; the Germans "answered with the same signal" and vanished. Kellett assumed both sides had challenged at once and took them for Laforey's division turning back [SRC 5]. The drifters of the 10th Division challenged, got no answer, and fired rifles [SRC 5]. At about 00:40 Nubian challenged destroyers she took for Lawford's division, turned away, and was shot up at once [SRC 5]. | Lights | German: "the rear destroyers turned on searchlights and opened fire" on the drifters (about 22:00) [SRC 5]. Nubian's petrol tanks burning "lit up the Straits", and other divisions steered for the flames [SRC 5]. A drifter fired rockets to raise the alarm [SRC 5] |
| Ranges | Nubian hit at "close range": "nearly every shot hit at point-blank range" [SRC 5]. Transport Queen stopped and boarded, then sunk [SRC 4, 5]. Few yard figures |
| Torpedoes and hits | Nubian torpedoed under the fore bridge while turning to ram the last German boat [SRC 5]. Amazon: each passing German boat fired a round and 2 hit (after gun out; 2 boilers in the after boiler room) [SRC 5]. Mohawk's helm jammed by a shell [SRC 5]. Viking not hit [SRC 5] |
| Losses | British: Flirt sunk (only the boat's crew survived), transport Queen, 6 drifters sunk, 3 drifters and a trawler damaged; Nubian wrecked (bow off, drifted ashore); Amazon damaged; 45 killed, 4 wounded and 10 taken prisoner in the drifters [SRC 4, 5]. German: one boat damaged at most [SRC 3] |
| Duration | About 22:00 to 01:00. Separate encounters of a few minutes each [SRC 5] |

**Mechanism.** The Germans saw first and were not seen (21:20). British ships challenged and took the reply, or no reply, for friends: Flirt got her own challenge echoed back. Every British challenge gave the Germans the identification and the first broadside. Flirt's searchlight, burned to rescue men, made her the target. Tribal divisions lost each other in the dark leaving harbour. A burning ship became a beacon for both sides.

### 1b. 23 November 1916 (the Downs)

Thirteen German destroyers left Zeebrugge, and six of them approached the Downs at slow speed just before 21:00 [SRC 5]. The armed drifter Acceptable saw six destroyers pass under her stern "at less than a cable" and recognised them as strangers: lighter paint and high forecastles [SRC 5]. Only the last German boat fired, 9-10 shells at Acceptable and Buckler, then a few rounds into Margate [SRC 5]. Bacon says Ramsgate was not shelled and nothing was sunk [SRC 4].

Light: moon 3% (a day before new) and below the horizon [INFERRED].

Mechanism: recognition by silhouette and paint at under 200 yd; the Germans held fire until the last boat was abeam.

### 1c. 25 February 1917 (Tillessen's and Albrecht's raid)

- **Light** [SRC 5]: "overcast and very dark, with the four-day-old moon hidden". Computed: moon 23%, age 4.2 d, alt -0.4° at 23:00 UTC, set 23:03 [INFERRED].
- **Sighting** [SRC 5]: at 22:30 Laverock, 3.5 mi SW of buoy 11a, sighted a destroyer on the port bow. "Moments later" it fired a red flare. The Germans had seen Laverock first and held fire until close range, then hit her with heavy fire.
- **Torpedo** [SRC 4]: a torpedo hit Laverock in her fresh-water tank and did not explode.
- **Blinding** [SRC 4]: the Germans' HE with time fuzes "blinded her bridge officers".
- **The lone ship read as a detachment** [SRC 5]: Laverock turned and passed through the rear of the German line. Tillessen took her reappearance on his quarter for three destroyers. He turned north about 22:40 and broke off about 23:00, intercepted her report, and gave up the attack on Dover.
- **Albrecht's group** [SRC 5]: it shelled the Downs anchorage, Margate and Broadstairs.
- **Date conflict** [UNCERTAIN]: Bacon [SRC 4] dates the Laverock raid 1 March. Newbolt's "four-day-old moon" fits 25 Feb (moon age 4.2 d), not 1 Mar (8.2 d, 62%, high at 37°) [INFERRED].

Mechanism: a single aggressive ship, unseen in detail, was read as a force and broke up a raid.

**Mechanism.** The challenge was the trigger: the challenger was torpedoed mid-signal. Rescue searchlights drew the raiders back from beyond the barrage, about 10 minutes after they were lit. The Germans attacked a lit ship with torpedoes only, so they gave no flashes away. Losses to unseen torpedoes were blamed on submarines.

### 1e. 20-21 April 1917: Swift and Broke (the bench case)

| Ranges | "Close range" or "so short" [SRC 6]. Swift passed through the German line [SRC 5]. Broke's last torpedo into G85 was fired at 200 yd (set to 6 ft) [SRC 6]. The bench's "about 600 yd" was not found in Evans, Bacon or Newbolt [UNCERTAIN] |
| Torpedoes | Swift fired at least 1, probably hitting G85 [SRC 4, 5]. Broke fired 3 (port foremost tube at G85, "We've got her"; a second at G85 taking her for another boat; a third at 200 yd sank G85) [SRC 4, 6]. Bacon credits at least one hit on G85 from the port torpedoes of both [SRC 4] |
| Ram | Swift turned hard to starboard to ram and missed [SRC 5]. Broke rammed G42 abreast the after funnel [SRC 5] or abaft it [SRC 4], at about 27 kn [SRC 7] (Bacon: both near 30 kn [SRC 4]). The collision swung both "nearly sixteen points" [SRC 4]. The ships locked, and Germans boarded Broke [SRC 1] |
| Guns | Broke's right-ahead broadside was 4 × 4 in, controlled from the bridge [SRC 6]. Two boats astern of G42 "poured heavy shell fire" into Broke while she was locked [SRC 5]. Swift's forecastle gun flash blinded Peck, and she lost the enemy for "several seconds" [SRC 4, 5] |

| Duration | Firing began 00:45. Broke's engines stopped about 01:20 [SRC 5]. Taken in tow about 01:15 per [SRC 5], but the source is internally inconsistent [UNCERTAIN]. Main melee: a few minutes [EST] |

**Mechanism.** In a moonless night the two sides met on opposite courses with no early warning. The raiders saw first and fired first, and the British identified them by their funnel glow about 10 s after the first shells. The British answer was instant: torpedo, then ram, with fire held "until the director sights bore" [SRC 6]. Own gun flash blinded the firing bridge. The rammer was helpless for a time and took fire from the boats astern. The Germans escaped "in the darkness" [SRC 6].

Bench check: "moonless, 2 against 6, 2 German boats sunk" is supported [SRC/INFERRED]. "About 600 yd" is not found. The best-sourced figures are point-blank passing ranges, a 200 yd final torpedo, and contact at about 10 s after the first enemy fire.

### 1f. 14-15 February 1918 (Heinecke's raid on the barrage)

- **Light** [SRC 8, INFERRED]: "very dark and hazy". Moon 19% crescent, set 21:54 UTC on 14 Feb, so moonless at 01:00.
- **Forces** [SRC 8]: German II Flotilla (Heinecke), 8 large boats, with G104 turning back. British: 58 drifters in 9 divisions over the deep minefield; West Barrage Patrol Swift and Marksman; East Barrage Patrol Termagant, Melpomene, Zubian and Amazon; monitor M26; P-boats and others.
- **The barrage's light** [SRC 8]: trawlers burned flares at random intervals, and a Folkestone searchlight swept. Illumination covered about 2.5 nm either side of the deep minefield. The drifters were to report with green Very lights. The lit barrage made the drifters visible targets [INFERRED].
- **Attack** [SRC 8]: Newbury was fired on at about 00:30 by two destroyers, about 50 yd off a paddle minesweeper. Then came the drifter divisions.
- **Losses** [SRC 8]: 7 drifters and the trawler James Pond sunk; Newbury damaged. Casualties are given as 89 killed (Newbolt, Dunn), or 22 killed, 54 drowned and 13 wounded (Hurd). German: G102 mined on the way home and repaired.
- **Misreadings** [SRC 8]: the gunfire was read as the hunting of a U-boat (a U-boat had been sighted 23:30-00:00), as an air raid, or as a monitor shooting. ML 12 thought the destroyers British. M26 saw the green lights but did not report.
- **Amazon on the way home** [SRC 13]: "the Germans repeatedly failed to respond to the identification challenge". Her crew "believed they were British" and reported British destroyers to Termagant too late.
- **Discipline** [SRC 8]: Amazon's captain was court-martialled; the captains of M26 and Termagant were disciplined.

Mechanism: an unanswered challenge did not lead to fire. Patrols expecting friends or U-boats held fire on unknowns, and the raiders walked through the barrage lit by its own flares.

## 2. The Adriatic and the Otranto Straits

### 2a. 29 December 1915 (the Durazzo raid; night run-in and twilight escape)

- **Monge** [SRC 10]: the raiding force sighted a periscope "at about 2:30 a.m." Monge was rammed and sunk; 2 officers and 25 men were taken prisoner, and the force resumed course an hour later.
- **Who rammed Monge** [UNCERTAIN]: Newbolt's paraphrased text says Balaton rammed her [SRC 10]; Wikipedia says Helgoland [SRC 15].
- **Moon at 02:30 CET** [INFERRED]: 55% waning, alt 30°, so moonlit.
- **Mines**: Lika sank on two mines off Durazzo and Triglav was mined, in daylight (the paraphrase's "8 pm" is evidently am) [SRC 10].
- **Dusk** [SRC 5 p.149]: Seitz's Austrians "stood out against the last glow of twilight". Weymouth and Nino Bixio straddled them, but within minutes their fire slackened "as gunlaying and range-finding became guesswork in the growing darkness".
- **Dusk timing** [INFERRED]: sunset 16:25 CET, nautical dusk 17:30 CET.
- **Italian destroyers** [SRC 10]: Mosto and Pilo made a last torpedo attempt at about 17:02 but could not get within torpedo range without coming under fire.

Mechanism: a ship against the afterglow is visible from the dark side. Within minutes of dusk, fire control fails before sight does.

### 2c. 9-10 December 1917 (MAS 9 and MAS 13 sink Wien at Trieste)

- Two MAS boats got through the harbour defences undetected. Torpedoes were fired at 02:32. MAS 9 (Rizzo) hit Wien with 2 torpedoes; those at Budapest missed. Wien had all watertight doors open and capsized in about 5 min; 46 killed. Both boats escaped unseen [SRC 16].
- Moon 19% waning crescent, below the horizon at 02:32 CET (alt -9°, rising 03:24 CET) [INFERRED].
- No lights were used by either side in the sources reached [SRC 16].
- Launch range was not reached [UNCERTAIN].

### 2d. 10 June 1918 (MAS 15 and MAS 21 at Premuda; Szent István)

- **Sighting** [SRC 17, 18]: about 03:15, a large smoke cloud about 6.5 mi away, abaft the starboard beam. Then two battleships with 8-10 destroyers.
- **Light** [SRC 18, INFERRED]: Rizzo chose "luce incerta" (uncertain light). Computed: sun -15° at 03:25 CEST (astronomical twilight); nautical dawn 03:54 CEST; sunrise 05:16 CEST. Moon 2% (a day after new), below the horizon, so effectively moonless.
- **Approach** [SRC 18]: at minimum speed, then 12 kn to pass between the escorts.
- **MAS 15** [SRC 17, 18]: launched at ≤300 m and hit with 2 torpedoes at 03:25 (Sieche gives 03:15, Sokol 03:30 [SRC 17]).
- **MAS 21** [UNCERTAIN, conflict]: launched at 450-500 m at Tegetthoff. Wikipedia: missed [SRC 17]. Italian Wikipedia: one hit, one late and passing astern [SRC 18].
- **Pursuit** [SRC 18]: Tb 76 chased MAS 15 and opened fire at 100-150 m; one of the two depth charges Rizzo dropped burst, and Tb 76 broke off.
- **Searchlights**: none mentioned [SRC 17, 18].
- **Loss** [SRC 17, 18]: Szent István capsized at 06:12, 2 h 47 min after the hits. 89 dead [SRC 17]; 14 dead and 75 missing [SRC 18].

Mechanism: in pre-dawn twilight a big ship's smoke was seen at 6.5 mi while a low MAS was unseen to under 300 m. The escort found the boat only after the launch.

## 3. The Baltic

### 3a. 16-17 August 1915 (V99 and V100 in the Gulf of Riga)

- **Mission and start** [SRC 19]: the boats were sent into the gulf to find the battleship Slava at night. They ran past the minefields under the Courland coast from 18:30.
- **About 20:00, dusk** [SRC 19, INFERRED]: a brief fight with 2 old Okhotnik-class destroyers, which broke off. Computed sun about -5° (civil twilight).
- **About 02:00, dark** [SRC 19, INFERRED]: SE of Zerel they met 2 Ukraina-class boats and fired torpedoes, which were evaded. Computed sun -12°; moon 38%, set before midnight, so moonless.
- **About 04:15, dawn** [SRC 19, INFERRED]: V99 met Novik and attacked; 3 Emir Bukharsky-class boats joined. V99 was hit several times, caught fire forward and amidships, and struck 2 mines escaping. Computed sun about -3°, sunrise about 03:52 CET.
- **Rescue** [SRC 19]: V100 laid smoke about 05:00. Posen drove the Russians off.
- **Loss** [SRC 19]: V99 sank at 08:00 off Pissen; 21 killed, and 22 wounded were saved by V100.
- **Not given** [SRC 19]: ranges and searchlights.

Mechanism: a night torpedo attack at an unseen target (Slava) failed to find her. The decisive gunnery came at dawn against a faster, better-armed destroyer.

## 4. The Dardanelles and the Black Sea

### 4a. Minesweeping under the searchlights, March 1915

- **Early March** [SRC 20]: Triumph fired on the searchlights after dark and kept them to a few seconds' burning at a time, but "never hit them". Sweeping was impossible.
- **Ships' guns against the lights** [SRC 20]: "the battleships kept the searchlights to occasional flashes at night", and the trawlers then cleared a channel part of the way.
- **Kephez, early March** [SRC 20]: Mosquito, 3 trawlers and 2 picket boats were "spotted immediately" by the searchlights, and the heavy fire forced a withdrawal.
- **10-11 Mar** [SRC 20, INFERRED]: 7 trawlers, 2 picket boats, 4 destroyers, Canopus and Amethyst. "The searchlights were out for twenty minutes" and the trawlers got above the first minefield. The first pair caught 2 mines; Manx Hero sank. "The searchlights came on right away" and 2 trawlers were hit by 6 in shells. Moon 24% waning, below the horizon (rose about 03:30 local).
- **11-12 Mar** [SRC 20]: "when the beams caught the leading trawler and shells fell, she turned about" and the rest followed. The French attempt the same night was hit several times and swept nothing.
- **13-14 Mar** [SRC 20, INFERRED]: volunteer crews. "Two powerful searchlights lit the approach". Fire came from Dardanos, Messudieh, Rumili and perhaps Yildiz (6 × 6 in). Only 2 trawlers could deploy sweeps. 4 trawlers and 1 picket boat were disabled. 27 killed and 43 wounded, nearly all in Amethyst, which exposed herself off Kephez Point to draw fire; 2 shells caused 60 casualties. The Admiral concluded night sweeping was too costly and went to daylight. Moon 4%, below the horizon, so a dark night.
- **Earlier picket-boat attack on the wreck of Saphir/E15** [SRC 20]: two beams caught both boats near the bend; a "brief lapse in the beam" allowed the launch.

Mechanism: coastal searchlights plus quick-firing and 6 in batteries make a lit zone ships cannot work in. Being lit brought fire within seconds, and small craft turned away when caught. Gunfire could make the lights flicker but not destroy them.

## 5. Coronel, 1 November 1914 (the night end)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Light | Sunset 18:50 ship's time; the Germans opened fire at sunset, 12,000 yd [SRC 21]. Moon 99% (full at 23:48 UTC on 2 Nov), rose about 18:10 ship's time, so it was low in the ENE at the start and about 27-30° high by 21:00 [INFERRED]. Newbolt: by 20:15 "the moon had risen above the clouds"; Nürnberg found Monmouth "in the dim moonlight" [SRC 22]. Spray and poor visibility slowed Glasgow's shooting [SRC 22] |
| Ranges in the twilight | Glasgow opened at 19:05 at about 10,000 yd; Good Hope turned toward the enemy at 19:35 at 5,500 yd; 4,600 yd at 19:52 [SRC 22] |
| Gunfire | Glasgow ceased about 20:00, after about an hour. Glasgow estimated about 600 shells fired at her, with 5 waterline hits [SRC 22]. Gneisenau fired 244 and Scharnhorst 422 rounds [SRC 21] |
| After dark | Monmouth, listing 10° to port, turned away and "vanished from German view". Glasgow saw German light cruisers in line abreast coming from the ESE by moonlight (von Spee had ordered torpedo attacks), and ran west [SRC 22] |
| Nürnberg and Monmouth | Nürnberg found Monmouth at about 21:00 [SRC 23] by steering for the gun flashes [SRC 22]. She directed her searchlights at Monmouth's ensign as an invitation to surrender [SRC 21]. She ceased fire to allow a surrender, the flag stayed up, and she reopened from the side on which Monmouth's port guns could not bear [SRC 22, 23]. Range 550-900 m [SRC 24]. Monmouth capsized about 21:20; 734 lost, no survivors [SRC 23] |

**Mechanism.** Twilight first favoured the side looking toward the afterglow (the British were outlined against it). After dark, a rising full moon behind cloud let the light cruisers find a cripple by her gun flashes. The searchlight was used for identification and surrender, not for gunnery, and only at under 1 km against a ship that could not reply.

### 6b. 31 October 1912 (Greek TB 11, Votsis, sinks Feth-i Bülend at Salonika)

- **Entry** [SRC 27]: passed the harbour defences at 22:20, "by the shore batteries and searchlights and through the mine barrages". Votsis "evaded the searchlights of Karaburnu fortress" [SRC 28].
- **Attack** [SRC 27, 28]: target sighted 23:20. Starboard torpedo at 23:35 from 150 m, then the port torpedo; a third, from the deck tube while turning, exploded on the quay [SRC 28]. Three torpedoes, 2 hits [SRC 27].
- **Loss** [SRC 27, 28]: the ship capsized and sank; 7 killed, most of the crew being ashore.
- **Escape** [SRC 27, 28]: by the same route without incident.
- **Moon** [INFERRED]: 64% waning gibbous, rose about 21:40 local, alt about 18° at 23:35, so a moonlit harbour. Local time is uncertain.

Mechanism: a small boat with a low silhouette passed sweeping fortress lights and launched at 150 m against an unready ship at anchor.

### 6c. 21 November 1912 (Bulgarian torpedo boats vs Hamidiye, "Kaliakra")

- **Forces** [SRC 29, 30]: Drazki, Letyashti, Smeli and Strogi against Hamidiye and 2 destroyers, about 32 mi off Varna.
- **Timing** [SRC 30]: shortly after midnight; torpedoes fired at 00:43.
- **Attack** [SRC 29, 30]: Letyashti led and missed; Smeli and Strogi missed. Smeli was hit by a 150 mm round, with 1 wounded. Drazki closed to about 100 m and hit the starboard bow.
- **Damage** [SRC 29]: a hole of about 10 m²; 8 killed and 30 wounded; calm sea. The ship got home.
- **Moon** [INFERRED]: 85% waxing gibbous, alt about 26° in the WSW at 00:43, setting about 03:20 local, so a moonlit attack.

How Hamidiye detected them, and whether she used searchlights, was not reached.

Mechanism: in moonlight the first three boats launched from too far and were seen and fired on. Only the boat that closed to about 100 m hit.

## 7. North Sea night destroyer actions

### 7a. 16 December 1914 (Warrender's destroyers vs the High Seas Fleet's screen, Scarborough raid)

- **Light** [SRC 20, 31, INFERRED]: the action ran from 05:15 GMT and was broken off "after a couple of hours in the dark". Computed: moon 1% and below the horizon; nautical dawn 06:37 GMT; sunrise 08:06 GMT.
- **First contact** [SRC 31]: at 05:15 Lynx sighted V155.
- **Hamburg and the destroyers** [SRC 20]: a cruiser appeared on the port beam of Hardy and Shark at 600-700 yd (the printed time "6.58" is garbled). Lynx "switched on recognition lights" and Hardy opened fire, followed by Shark. The cruiser turned on searchlights and returned heavy fire. Later the cruiser "relit her searchlights" and reopened fire. Hardy replied at under 500 yd and fired a torpedo; "her lights went out" and the cruiser withdrew south. Hardy was severely damaged and Ambuscade holed; Lynx's propeller was damaged [SRC 20, 31].
- **The reverse challenge** [SRC 20]: three German cruisers challenged Lynx. She answered "with a signal similar to one she had seen a German destroyer use", and they steamed off.

Mechanism: a cruiser's searchlights won the first exchange at 500-700 yd but made her the torpedo target. A copied enemy reply passed a challenge.

### 7b. 22-23 January 1917 (Harwich Force vs the German 6th Flotilla; Simoom)

- **Light** [SRC 5, INFERRED]: "fine and clear with a light easterly breeze", hard frost, fully dark. Computed: moon 0% and below the horizon.
- **Forces** [SRC 5, 32]: Tyrwhitt had 6 light cruisers, 2 leaders and 16 destroyers. The German 6th Flotilla (Schultz) had V69 and 10 boats.
- **About 02:45** [SRC 5]: Centaur's division in line ahead. Conquest, the rear ship, first saw the Germans crossing astern. All ships fired, but "neither side used searchlights, since each was visible from the other's funnel flare". The German fire was "vigorous but inaccurate". Tyrwhitt swerved to avoid a torpedo. The Germans escaped behind smoke. V69 was hit, her helm jammed, and G41 rammed her; G41 fell to 8 kn. S50 lost touch.
- **Later** [SRC 5]: Penelope's group met the crippled V69 alone on a reciprocal course. 3 cruisers switched on searchlights and fired "at very close range". The lights went off too soon to confirm a sinking. V69 reached IJmuiden with a funnel down and a shell hole aft.
- **About 04:00** [SRC 5]: S50, alone, ran into the British patrols near the Schouwen Bank, concentrated fire on the leading Simoom, and torpedoed her; her magazine exploded. 47 died [SRC 32]. She was sunk next morning by Nimrod [SRC 5, 32]. The 6th Flotilla had passed the Simoom group "unseen" at about 03:53 [SRC 5].

Mechanism: funnel flare made searchlights unnecessary at close range. Searchlights were used on a lone cripple. A single boat attacking the head of a line from the dark side sank the leader.

## Patterns

1. **The side that sees first fires first, and in these cases it was nearly always the raider** [SRC 1a, 1c, 1e, 7b]. On 26 Oct 1916 the Germans saw British destroyers at 21:20 without being seen. At Laverock and at Swift and Broke the British came under fire before, or seconds before, they identified anything.
2. **Challenging was usually fatal for the challenger** [SRC 1a, 1d, 2b]. Paragon was torpedoed before her challenge was finished. Nubian, Amazon and Viking were fired on as they challenged, and Borea as she made her recognition signal at about 1,000 m. The raiders never challenged first; they treated every ship as hostile.
3. **Holding fire on an unknown cost the defender the night** [SRC 1a, 1f]. Flirt took the reply as friendly. In 1918 Amazon took an unanswered challenge as British. Patrols read gunfire as U-boat hunting or an air raid. Bacon's own conclusion: fire "on all vessels without challenge" in raid areas [SRC 4].
4. **The challenge can be spoofed** [SRC 1a, 7a]. The Germans echoed Flirt's challenge back (1916). In 1914 Lynx passed three German cruisers by repeating a signal she had seen a German destroyer make.
5. **Searchlights drew fire and torpedoes** [SRC 1a, 1d, 7a]. Flirt and Llewellyn were attacked within minutes of lighting rescue searchlights; Tillessen came back 10 min after seeing Laforey's light. Hamburg's searchlights at under 500-700 yd brought a torpedo, and her "lights went out".
6. **Shore searchlights with guns made a zone ships could not work in** [SRC 4a]. Being caught turned trawlers about. Ships' fire made the lights flicker but did not destroy them.
7. **Funnel flare and gun flash identified ships at close range without lights** [SRC 1e, 7b]. In 1917 neither side used searchlights "since each was visible from the other's funnel flare". The flash of one's own guns blinded the bridge for seconds (Swift; Laverock by HE bursts).
8. **Engagement ranges in the dark were point-blank** [SRC]. Under 1 cable, 50-200 yd, 500-700 yd for a cruiser with searchlights, 1,000 m for a recognition signal. Torpedo boats at anchored or unready ships launched at 100-300 m. A ram became a real option.
9. **Moonless nights were chosen for raids** [INFERRED + SRC 4]. 26 Oct 1916 (new moon), 23 Nov 1916, 25 Feb 1917 (moon hidden), 17 Mar 1917 (moon below), 20 Apr 1917 (new), 14 Feb 1918 (set). Bacon: raids came at dark periods; coastal motor boats wanted first or last quarter moons, since bright moon was "near daylight" [SRC 4].
10. **Twilight at the target's back** [SRC 2a, 5]. The ship against the afterglow is seen; within minutes of full dark, gunnery became "guesswork". A rising moon lets cruisers find a cripple by her gun flashes (Coronel).
11. **Smoke betrays big ships before small ones** [SRC 2d]. At Premuda, smoke was seen at 6.5 mi in twilight, while the MAS got within 300 m unseen.
12. **A lone, aggressive ship is over-counted** [SRC 1c]. Laverock was taken for three destroyers, and the raid was abandoned.
13. **Lit ships rarely turned away; small craft did** [SRC 4a, 7a]. The sweepers turned about when caught by the beams. Hardy hauled out of line under a cruiser's lights but came back. A cruiser whose lights drew a torpedo shut them and withdrew.
