## Summary

1. **A ship was given up when she could not hurt the enemy, could not get away, and an enemy was in reach.** The
   recurring states are: no gun firing or only one or two left (Vizcaya, Oquendo, Emden, Königsberg), the last
   ammunition gone (Leipzig, Gneisenau), a fire beyond control because the mains or pumps were lost (María Teresa,
   Oquendo, Vizcaya, Reina Cristina), steering lost (Reina Cristina, Varyag, Rurik, Mainz), or stopped with flooding
   beyond the pumps (Sissoi, Nürnberg, Lützow, Elbing). Two of these together, with an enemy close, gave a decision
   within about 5-60 minutes by day. **[SRC]** cases, **[INFERRED]** the rule [A][B]
2. **Casualties were not the trigger.** Killed and wounded at the decision ran about 7-50% of the crew (Nikolai I ~7%,
   Sissoi ~7% killed, Oryol ~14%, Lützow ~14%, Mainz 20%, Königsberg ~20%, Varyag 23-39%, Emden ~40-50%, Reina Cristina
   "half"). Ships were given up when they could not fight, not when the men were gone. **[INFERRED]** [A][B]
3. **Two clocks.** In action the decision followed the last gun or the critical damage within about 5-50 minutes
   (median about 20). A cripple out of the fight, with a friend alongside or alone at night, was nursed for hours (2-14 h:
   Lützow ~7, Warrior ~14, Sissoi ~11, Monomakh and Nakhimov 10-12) and given up when the water won or an enemy came in
   sight; then within 0-10 minutes (Elbing, Rostock, Sparrowhawk). **[INFERRED]** [A][B]
4. **The end chosen depended on place and navy more than state.** Land in reach: beach, then burn or flood (every
   Spanish ship at Santiago; the Yalu's burning small cruisers; Emden; Donskoi). Open sea and sinking: scuttle or
   abandon (Rurik, Ushakov, Mainz, Cöln, Gneisenau, Leipzig). Strike only rarely: to save the men of a sinking ship
   (Sissoi), when stopped and still fired on (Nürnberg), or in minor and beaten navies (Avnillah 1912). Base falling:
   scuttle in harbour (Port Arthur, Weihaiwei, Sevastopol in 55 m). **[SRC]** [A][B][E]
5. **A sound ship was surrendered only by an admiral, or with one aboard.** Nebogatov's four (over most of his
   officers' objections), Bedovy (the wounded Rozhestvensky and his staff aboard) and Weihaiwei (written in the dead
   admiral's name). Captains of sound ships fled (Jiyuan, Guangjia, Izumrud) or interned (the Yellow Sea breakouts);
   Colón, with dummy main guns and failing coal, is the one captain's strike of a near-intact ship. **[SRC]** [A][F]
6. **Germans and British in 1914-18 almost never struck.** They fought the last gun, then abandoned or scuttled with
   the colours up (Königsberg, Leipzig, Monmouth, Blücher, Shark). The German exceptions are Nürnberg (stopped, silent,
   fired on five more minutes, then struck), Emden (struck hours after beaching, once fired on again) and the launch
   Kingani (captain killed, struck by her engineer). No British strike was found. **[SRC]** [B][C]
7. **The sign that counted was the ensign hauled down, every one of them, on a ship that had stopped and gone quiet.**
   A white flag strictly asked for a parley; a code hoist alone was not trusted; Tōgō kept firing until Nebogatov's
   ships stopped their engines. Fire stopped at once or within about 5 minutes of a clear sign (Santiago, Kent on
   Nürnberg); a doubtful sign (one ensign of two, a hoist only, still moving) took 10-30 minutes (Gneisenau 20 min,
   Nebogatov "tens of minutes" **[UNCERTAIN]**). A gun firing again cancelled the cease-fire (Gneisenau). **[SRC]** [C][F]
8. **Fire after a sign came from incomplete signs, not malice.** Emden (colours up after beaching, two salvoes, ~20
   killed), Nürnberg (colours up, 5 min more), Gneisenau (fired again). Proven fire on men who had surrendered is rare
   in the sources reached: Baralong (1915) is the clear case, and Ushakov's men were fired on in the water before the
   rescue. No sourced case of a ship taken for struck when she was not. **[SRC]** [C][B][A]
9. **Who lived was set by the mode of loss and by whether a rescuer was near and free, more than by the ship's size.**
   Blew up: 0-2% saved. Capsized in under ~15 min with no rescuer within ~1 nm: 2-20%. The same with craft within a few
   hundred metres: 50-93%. 15 min to 2.5 h with rescue late, cold or rough: 20-40%. Over 1 h with friends alongside:
   80-100%. Over 1 h with no rescue (still under fire, alone): 0-5%. **[INFERRED]** from about 30 cases with
   figures read from sources and the rest of D1's table from memory [D1]
10. **Cold water decided the rest.** About half of men in 10 °C water die within an hour (Robertson) **[SRC]**; at the
    Falklands (6-8 °C **[EST]**) Gneisenau gave 20-22% saved, Leipzig 18 of 288, Nürnberg 7 of 334, and a share of
    those picked up from 5-10 °C water died after rescue (Nürnberg 4 of 11, Invincible's Gneisenau men 14 of 108)
    **[SRC]** [D2]. In 20 °C and warmer water nearly all who reached it and were looked for lived (Rurik ~81%, Szent
    István ~91%). **[INFERRED]** [D1][D2]
11. **Scuttling time is set by the openings made, not by size.** Charges: 10-30 min (Dresden ~30, Cöln ~14 from the
    hits). A consort's torpedoes into a flooding ship: 2-30 min (Lützow 2 min after she was awash, Rostock ~30 with
    charges, Svobodnaya Rossiya 4 min after the fourth), with many failures (Slava 1 of 6 worked). Valves alone at
    Scapa: 56-340 min for capital ships, median about 2 h 40, first list visible only after ~40 min. Shallow water
    meant a raisable wreck (Varyag, Novik, the Port Arthur ships); Essen chose 55 m. **[SRC]** times, **[INFERRED]**
    medians [E][B]
12. **The victor's choices were prize, finish, leave or rescue.** A whole, stopped, struck ship was taken (Nebogatov's
    four, Bedovy, Spartak, Avtroil) and steaming or towed home the same day; a sinking one was lost under the prize crew
    (Sissoi, 1 h 50 min after surrender); a scuttled one was lost when moved (Colón). A wreck with colours up was
    finished, usually by torpedo, which often needed a second (Blücher, Mainz, Wiesbaden). A cripple nobody could reach
    safely was left (Wiesbaden 7-8 h between the fleets; the Admiralty's 22 Sept 1914 order "the disabled ship must be
    left to her fate"). **[SRC]** [F]
13. **Rescue held the victor's ships for tens of minutes under threat and for hours without.** Two auxiliaries took
    613 off Sissoi in at most 110 min (~170 men a ship-hour **[INFERRED]**); Sydney took ~5 h for Emden's men through
    surf; L5's bombs ended the British rescue at Blücher; Hogue and Cressy were torpedoed while stopped for Aboukir's
    men. Hague X (1907) binds a search for survivors only "so far as military interests permit". **[SRC]** [F][D2]
14. **The written rules give a usable test.** Peter's 1720 Ustav allows a strike only on a council's signed decision
    when the leak is beyond the pumps, the powder is spent in fighting, and no shoal is near to run her on; the 1906
    courts read it so (Nebogatov's three intact ships' captains condemned, Oryol's officers acquitted). The RN's 1866
    Act and the US Articles punish surrender "when capable of making a successful defence" or "without proper
    authority". Neither demands scuttling: that was culture and doctrine. **[SRC]** [A][B]

- **Every strike or scuttling at sea had an enemy in sight and in reach** (about 6,000 m for Sissoi, about 12,000 m under
  fire for Nebogatov, 2,700 m for Nürnberg, "very close" for Vizcaya, a destroyer alongside for Bedovy). Damage alone,
  with no enemy, led to holding on and making for land or home. The exceptions fled at the start (Jiyuan, Guangjia).
  **[INFERRED]** [A]
- **Night stretches the clock:** a ship hit at night and left alone decided at or after dawn, 10-12 h on, when she
  reached the coast or was found (Sissoi, Monomakh, Nakhimov, Donskoi). **[INFERRED]** [A]
- **Who decided:** the captain, or whoever was left (Rurik's 13th officer, Mainz's XO, Kingani's engineer, Vizcaya's XO
  with Eulate's standing order). Spanish captains polled their officers, matching Peter's council. Admirals decided to
  **leave** a ship (Hipper and Blücher; Spee's "save yourself" to Gneisenau) but in 1914-18 never to strike one. A
  committee overrode the captain only in the revolutionary Russian fleet (Slava). **[INFERRED]** [A][B]
- **A consort finished a ship already emptied** (G38 for Lützow, V71 and V73 for Rostock, Marksman for Sparrowhawk, the
  destroyers for Slava, Kerch at Novorossiysk). **[SRC]** [B]

### 3. Abandoning ship and who lived

#### 3.1 Boats, floats and launching

- **Boats for well under the whole crew, often unusable in action.** Aboukir launched one boat: blast damage and the

  Jutland **[UNCERTAIN]**. [D2]

## Recommendations for this codebase

All numbers below are suggestions, tagged with what they rest on; Claude Code decides what to build. They're written for
the step balance.md names as next after BL5's proposals 1-3: "striking colours and abandoning ship", the captain's
call (crew.md decision 3), with the captain's temper carrying most of it (crew.md decision 7: "the captain is the
critical thing whose personality matters the most") and a navy's practice as doctrine set per scenario from the record
(decision 10), never period flags on ships.

### R1. The triggers: an end of ship read from `Fitness` and the picture

A captain judges his ship's end alongside `FallOutRule`, from his own `Fitness` (known exactly) and his navy's picture.
Four pressures, each 0-1 and smooth (no hard switch), each with the cases it's fitted to:

| Pressure | Read from | Suggested shape | Fitted to | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Can't hurt** | `Fitness` firepower (main and secondary, weighted by calibre), rounds left, torpedoes left in working tubes | 0 above 0.3 of her guns; 1 at 0.1 or less, or rounds spent; linear between | Emden and Königsberg at 1 gun of 10; Oquendo 2 × 14 cm; Vizcaya, Leipzig, Gneisenau at none | [EST] |
| **Can't stay afloat** | `FloodModel`'s net inflow against pumps and the reserve left: the captain's own time-left estimate | 1 when time left < time to get the men off (R4) + 10 min; 0 above ~2 h | Hogue's order within 5 min; Lützow (8,000 t, screws lifting); Warrior (deck 1.2 m above a rising sea) | [EST] |
| **Can't get away** | `Fitness` speed against the enemy's seen speed; stopped; steering | 1 stopped, or steering lost with an enemy closing; scaled by the speed deficit | Reina Cristina, Varyag, Rurik, Mainz (steering); Sissoi, Nürnberg (stopped); Colón (coal) | [EST] |
| **Fire beyond control** | `Fires`: burning spaces no party is fighting (mains or pumps out), and their nearness to a magazine | 1 with a magazine threatened and no water | María Teresa, Oquendo, Vizcaya, Reina Cristina | [EST] |
| **The enemy** | the picture: an enemy in reach of his guns, or closing within ~10-12 km by day; at night only one sighted | multiplies the rest; with none, the ship holds on (R2, "hold") | every strike and scuttling at sea had one in sight [A] | [INFERRED] |

She becomes a candidate when "can't get away" and the enemy are both up and either "can't hurt", "can't stay afloat" or
"fire beyond control" is high (the record's "two of the four with an enemy close" [A]), or when "can't stay afloat"
alone is high whatever the enemy (abandoning a sinking ship needs no enemy). **Crew losses are not a trigger** (7-50% at
the decision [A][B]); the men count only through the guns they work (crew.md N2), which "can't hurt" already reads.

### R4. What an observer sees, and when he believes it

- **A new seen fact, colours down** (`SeenFlags`, beside burning, low, listing; read by the sightings, never the
  drawing). Seen only where the observer could read a flag: suggest within the range she's made out (the class taken
  for) by day, and at night only lit or within about 1 km **[EST]**; the record has no sourced distance **[UNCERTAIN]**
  [C]. Every ensign must come down (Gneisenau's peak flag); a flag shot away isn't struck.
- **A white flag** as a weaker sign: a parley. It slows fire under a low `Trust` and stops it under a high one
  (Santiago's Texas ceased; Tōgō didn't) **[SRC]** [C].
- **Believed** = colours down + stopped as seen (the estimated speed under `DoneForKn`, 4 kn) + silent as seen
  (`Sightings.Quiet`, past a short quiet) **[INFERRED]** from Tōgō's and Kent's practice [C]. Then fire stops within 0-5
  min for a clear sign, 10-30 min for a partial one (one ensign, a hoist, still moving), scaled by `Trust` **[EST]** from
  Kent's ~5, Gneisenau's 20 and Nebogatov's tens of minutes.
- **A sign of life cancels it at once:** a salvo of hers seen (N5b's rule, as Gneisenau's case had it), or her way
  getting on (Izumrud) **[SRC]** [C].
- **Colours still up on a stopped, silent wreck** keep her a target under `Wrecks`/`Finish` as now (Nürnberg drew 5
  more minutes; Emden two salvoes) **[SRC]**.
- **The captains' pick:** a struck and believed ship's worth in `CaptainLayer.Matters` and `Worth` goes to nothing; the
  screens pass her (BL5 decision 1: "unless the cripple strikes their colors"); fire on her is logged as an incident,
  never chosen (Baralong as the record's warning) **[INFERRED]**.
- A request for later: the demand and answer (Sydney's "Do you surrender?" and no signal books) [C].

### R5. Survivors

Count the men of the crew model (crew.md N2) where they stand when the order comes, then three stages:

1. **Got off**, by space and by time to **capsize** (not to sink), §3.2's shares and throughput **[EST]**; boats usable
   by power and damage and list (§3.1); a magazine explosion leaves 0-2% (§3.4) **[INFERRED]**.
2. **In the water**, by sea temperature (§3.5's table and modifiers **[INFERRED]/[EST]**). The weather should carry a sea
   temperature by area and month (§3.5's list **[EST]**) if it doesn't already: `-weather` has `air_over_sea_k`, so the
   sea's may be there to read.
3. **Picked up**, by who is near and free: rates in §3.6 **[INFERRED]/[EST]**; deaths after pickup 10-35% from 5-10 °C
   water **[INFERRED]**; men not reached by the scenario's end counted as lost (Wiesbaden's raft) **[INFERRED]**.

The regime table (§3.4) is the check; ship size should drop out by itself.

### R6. Scuttling as physics

A scuttle is the crew opening holes into the cell graph, so `FloodModel` gives the time (balance.md decision P5:
"an effect goes in for the physics' sake"): sea cocks and condenser inlets as openings sized by the class (Q = Cd·A·√(2gh)
**[EST]**, 2,000-20,000 t/h at Scapa **[INFERRED]**), watertight doors left open, one side's valves for a list (Essen);
charges as holes plus, where a charge is in a magazine, `Magazines.SetOff`'s path (Dresden); a consort's torpedoes as
`Underwater` hits with a dud chance (Slava 1 of 6, Kerch 4) **[SRC]**. Opening takes minutes with the crew at the valves,
an hour or more unprepared **[EST]**. A scuttling ship towed or boarded late goes down anyway (Colón). Shallow water
leaves a raisable wreck (salvage is the campaign's) **[INFERRED]**.

**The end** (command.md "The end"; balance.md BL5 answer 4): a ship stops holding "every side done" and "quiet" open
when she's struck and believed (prize or not), abandoned, scuttling, sunk, or beached and abandoned. A stopped cripple
with colours up and men aboard still holds it, as the user asked, but the step makes her decide within R3's clocks, so
the hold ends through the system and not a hack. Men in the water hold nothing open except a rescue task that's been
given. **[INFERRED]**

## Open questions

- **German orders** on not striking and on scuttling (the Kaiser's or Admiralstab's 1914 instructions, standing charges
  aboard, the Dienstvorschriften): not found. Leads: Groos and Raeder, *Der Krieg zur See*. [B][E]
- **Russian texts:** the 1899 Ustav articles cited in 1906, Rurik's and Varyag's reports, Kostenko's surrender chapter,
  the Tsemes Bay accounts; ru.wikipedia and tsushima.su failed to load. [A][E]
- **British destroyer figures at Jutland** (Nestor's and Nomad's prisoners, Tipperary's dead): Campbell's *Jutland*, the
  1920 Despatches. [B][D1]
- **Prize-crew sizes and tow speeds:** Corbett's *Maritime Operations in the Russo-Japanese War*, the Japanese official
  history, Engadine's report. [F]
- **How far a struck ensign could be read**, and how often smoke or range delayed a cease-fire. [C]
- **Boat establishments by navy** (seats as a share of crew), boats landed before action, issue of lifebelts and
  floats; RN Seamanship Manual drill timings. [D2]
- **McCance et al. (MRC 291, 1956)** and Molnar (1946): deaths in the sinking, in boats and in the water by sea
  temperature, to replace §3.5's estimates. [D2]
- **Valve and condenser-inlet sizes** by class (Breyer, Gröner); Scapa's destroyer times (Canmore, van der Vat). [E]
- The US Naval War Code (1900), Oppenheim, the German Prisenordnung (1909) and the RN Prize Manual on a surrendered
  warship. [C][F]
- Not covered: the Greco-Turkish war 1919-22, Otranto 1917-18 warships, the Baltic and Danube civil-war cases. [B]
