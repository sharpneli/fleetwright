# Striking colours, beaching, abandoning and scuttling, 1890-1905: the decision and the ship's state

Scope: warships that could no longer fight, or judged they could not, 1890-1905. Each case is put into the same columns so it can be reduced to ranges by type and period. This note builds on `crews-under-fire.md` §1.1-1.2 (CUF) and does not repeat what it says about how the crews behaved under fire. It is about the end-of-ship decision.

Tags: [SRC] a source fetched this session says it (linked). [INFERRED] worked out from sourced figures. [EST] a general reference figure that was not re-verified this session; displacements are all [EST], to about ±10%. [UNCERTAIN] sources conflict, or the figure comes from background knowledge that couldn't be checked this session. These are listed so the writer can see what's missing, and are not to be cited as found.

Abbreviations: k/w = killed/wounded; "share" = (k+w)/crew unless stated.

## Q1. The cases: what each ship had left, who was near, who decided, what, and how fast

### Takeaway
Thirty-odd endings fall into six patterns:
1. Beach-and-burn next to a lee shore (the Spanish at Santiago, the Chinese small cruisers at the Yalu).
2. Scuttle at sea when the ship is beaten, sinking and alone (Rurik, Ushakov, Donskoi, Monomakh, Nakhimov, Novik, Varyag).
3. Strike to save the crew of an already-sinking ship (Sissoi Veliky, Maria Teresa, Oquendo).
4. Strike an intact or nearly intact ship facing hopeless odds (Nebogatov's four, Bedovy, Cristóbal Colón).
5. Run for a neutral port and intern (the Yellow Sea breakouts).
6. Scuttle or blow up in harbour when the base falls (Port Arthur, Weihaiwei, Cavite).

The only ships that surrendered with most of their fighting power intact were under an admiral's order or carried an admiral. Everywhere else the captain, his successor or a council decided, usually within 10-60 minutes of the ship losing her last guns, her steering or her ability to stay afloat.

### Inferences
- **The beach is the first choice where there's a coast.** Every Spanish ship at Santiago that decided anything beached first and struck or burned her flag afterwards. Eulate's standing order was "not to surrender the ship but rather beach or burn her". The Yalu's burning small cruisers beached too, and Jingyuan ran for the shallows. Away from land, the same state leads to scuttling (Rurik, Ushakov) or to striking (Sissoi).
- **What sets a damaged ship's decision**, from the sourced cases:
  - (a) no gun can fire, or only 2 of the 14 cm (Vizcaya, Oquendo);
  - (b) a fire beyond control because the mains or pumps are gone (María Teresa, Oquendo, Vizcaya, Reina Cristina; the magazines' threat is said in so many words);
  - (c) the steering lost (Reina Cristina, Varyag, Rurik);
  - (d) flooding past saving or stopped dead (Sissoi, Monomakh, Nakhimov, Novik).
  
  Two of the four together, with the enemy still in reach, gave a decision **within about 25-60 minutes** (Vizcaya 25 min; María Teresa and Oquendo under 1 h; Reina Cristina under 30 min) [INFERRED].
- **Night stretches the clock.** A ship hit at night and left alone (Sissoi, Monomakh, Nakhimov, Donskoi) decided at or after dawn, 10-12 h on, once the coast was reached or an enemy came in sight. Without an enemy in sight, a flooding ship fights the water and heads for land. The decision comes when an enemy appears, or when she can no longer move [INFERRED].
- **The enemy's presence is necessary, not damage alone.** Every strike or scuttling happened with an enemy in sight and closing (6,000 m for Sissoi; about 12,000 m for Nebogatov; Sazanami for Bedovy; "very close" for Vizcaya). Jiyuan and Guangjia are the exception: they fled at the start.
- **Surrender of a sound ship needs either an admiral or no fight at all.** Nebogatov's 4, Bedovy (the staff aboard) and Weihaiwei (the fleet command after the admiral's suicide) were command-level decisions. Colón is the one captain's surrender of an undamaged ship, and she had no main guns, slow coal and no friends left. Single captains with sound ships fled (Jiyuan, Guangjia, Izumrud) or ran for neutral ports (Yellow Sea); they didn't strike.
- **Crew losses at the decision are modest.** Killed and wounded as a share of the crew at the decision: Nikolai I about 7%, Oryol about 14%, Sissoi about 7% killed, Varyag 23-39%, Vizcaya about 25% or more (98 missing plus wounded of about 500 [crew EST]), Reina Cristina "half". Except Reina Cristina, none of these crews was close to being wiped out. Ships are given up when they can't fight, not when the men are gone [INFERRED].
- **Time to decision, rough range by situation:**

  | Situation | Time to decision |
  |---|---|
  | In action, by day, land near | 10-60 min |
  | In action, at sea, alone, steering gone | 1-5 h (Rurik) |
  | Torpedoed at night | 6-12 h, at dawn or when an enemy appears |
  | Intact, surrounded (admiral) | Minutes once under fire, after hours shadowed |
  | Base falling | Days to weeks |

## Q2. The written rules and the courts that applied them

### Takeaway
The Russian rule goes back to Peter I's 1720 Morskoi Ustav, Book III, ch. I, art. 90. A captain must never give up his ship "under pain of life and honour". The commentary allows surrender **only** on a council of all officers that signs the decision, to save the men, and only if (1) the leak is beyond the pumps, (2) the powder and shot are spent in fighting, and (3) no shoal is near to put her on. The 1906 courts drew the same line. Nebogatov and the captains of the three nearly intact ships were sentenced to death (commuted to 10 years). Oryol's officers, whose ship was judged "incapable of combat", were acquitted. Bedovy's four officers were sentenced to death, commuted to dismissal, and the wounded Rozhestvensky was acquitted. Elsewhere the rule was harsher (Fang executed for fleeing) or lenient (Essen decorated for scuttling).

### Cited Findings
- **Morskoi Ustav 1720, Book III, ch. I, art. 90**: "Мужественно поступать во время боя ... и не отдавать корабля неприятелю, кроме указных случаев"; the captain "не должен корабля неприятелю отдать ни в каком случае, под потерянием живота и чести." The commentary ("Толк") allows surrender only after a council of all officers and warrant officers signs the decision, to save the crew, when:
  1. "Ежели так пробит будет, что пумпами одолеть лекажи или течи не возможно";
  2. "Ежели пороху и аммуниции весьма ничего не станет" (spent in firing, not wasted);
  3. "Ежели во обеих вышеписанных нуждах никакой мели близко не случится" (no shoal near to run onto).
  
  — [Морской устав 1720, constitutions.ru](https://constitutions.ru/?p=11307)
- **Nebogatov's trial**: he and 77 subordinate officers were tried by court-martial; the trial began 22 Nov 1906 [SRC: Topwar] (December 1906 per WP). ### Inferences
- Peter's three conditions come close to a rule a game could use for when a strike is legal. The leak can't be held, the ammunition is gone, and no shoal is in reach, with all of that decided by a council. The 1906 courts read them that way: Oryol, battered past fighting, was acquitted, while the three nearly intact ships and the intact Bedovy were condemned. Rule (3) also explains why beaching came first: if a shoal is in reach, you put her on it.
- Every navy here treated **the flag and the means of fighting** (breech plugs, codes) as what must not reach the enemy. Hence burned flags, hulls flooded after grounding, Kingstons opened. Keeping the ship from being salvaged mattered too: Essen chose 55 m of water, and Novik was scuttled to make salvage impossible.
- Punishment was ordered by how intact the ship was given up. Fleeing intact (Fang) was death, carried out. Striking intact (Nebogatov's captains, Bedovy) was death, commuted. Striking a wreck (Oryol) was acquittal. Scuttling (Essen) was a decoration.

## Q3. Synthesis: the triggers, reduced for a game

### Takeaway
A captain gives up his ship when (A) she can't hurt the enemy, (B) she can't escape (stopped, no steering, too slow, cornered), and (C) an enemy is in reach or closing. Which end he picks depends on where she is and who commands. Land in reach: beach, then burn or flood. Open sea and sinking: scuttle, or strike if the enemy is near enough to rescue the men. Intact but hopeless: only an admiral strikes, a captain flees or interns. A base falling: scuttle in deep water if possible.

### Cited Findings (the pattern's anchors, all from the tables above)
- Can't hurt the enemy: Vizcaya "could no longer fire with a single gun" 25 min before grounding ([Eulate](https://spanamwar.com/vizcayaeulate.html)); Oquendo had 2 × 14 cm left and her hoists failed ([Cervera](https://spanamwar.com/cerverareport.htm)); Varyag reported most guns out ([WP Chemulpo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Chemulpo_Bay)).
- Fire beyond control because the mains or pumps are lost: María Teresa, Oquendo, Vizcaya ([Cervera](https://spanamwar.com/cerverareport.htm); [Eulate](https://spanamwar.com/vizcayaeulate.html)); Reina Cristina "before the magazines should explode" ([Montojo](https://history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/s/selected-documents-of-the-spanish-american-war/official-spanish-report-on-battle-of-manila-bay.html)); Sissoi's firefighting water cut ([WP Sissoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_battleship_Sissoi_Veliky)).
- Can't escape: Reina Cristina, Varyag and Rurik lost their steering; Sissoi was stopped; Colón's coal failed ([sources above](https://www.spanamwar.com/colon.htm)).
- Enemy in reach: about 6,000 m (Sissoi), about 12,000 m under fire (Nebogatov), "very close" (Vizcaya), destroyer alongside (Bedovy).

### Inferences
A shape for the step [INFERRED, for the designer to tune]:

1. **Eligibility.** A ship becomes a candidate when two of these hold:
   - no main or secondary gun bears or works (or under about 15% left);
   - a fire not being fought, with the mains down;
   - steering lost, or stopped;
   - flooding past what the pumps can hold, or GM or reserve buoyancy going.
   
   AND an enemy is within his gun range, or closing within about 10-12 km by day. At night with no enemy in sight, the ship holds on or makes for land until dawn or until she's found.
2. **Choice by place.**
   - Coast within reach at her speed (Santiago: up to about 75 mi for Colón, under 1 nm for Vizcaya): beach, then fire, flood or let the magazines go. The flag is struck or burned only once she's aground.
   - Otherwise, sinking: scuttle. If she's sinking and an enemy can take her men off, strike (Sissoi: the white flag after she asked for help).
   - Not sinking, enemy closing: scuttle, with the Russians in 1904-05 (Rurik, Novik, Ushakov) or strike (Spain at Santiago once she couldn't escape).
3. **Who.** The captain. If he's down, the next surviving officer, however junior (Rurik's 13th; Vizcaya's XO). Most Spanish decisions polled the officers, matching Peter's council. An intact ship struck only on an **admiral's order** (Nebogatov) or with a **wounded admiral and staff aboard** (Bedovy). A captain's own temper decides the sound-ship escapes: Jiyuan and Guangjia flee at the first fire; Izumrud runs the ring; the Yellow Sea ships intern.
4. **Navy and period temper.**
   - Beiyang 1894: early flight, then fights to the death (Zhiyuan), then suicide rather than surrender (Ding, Liu).
   - Spain 1898: beach, burn and never give up the flag; Colón struck sound.
   - Russia 1904-05: scuttle first; strike sound ships only under an admiral; refusal common (Ushakov, Izumrud).
   - Japan: no case.
5. **Clocks.** About 10-60 min by day in close action with land near. 1-5 h at sea. 6-12 h after a night torpedo. Days to weeks when a base falls (Sevastopol's 15 days, triggered by the fortress, not the ship).
6. **Crew state is not the trigger.** The k+w share at the decision ran 7-40%, about 50% only at Reina Cristina. A crew-loss threshold alone would miss most cases. Gun, fire, steering, flooding and the enemy's nearness carry it.

### Gaps
- Too few cases per type to give real ranges by type. Battleships: Nikolai I, Oryol, Sissoi, Sevastopol, Dingyuan/Zhenyuan. Armoured cruisers: the Spanish 4, Rurik, Donskoi, Nakhimov, Monomakh. Protected cruisers and smaller: the rest. Destroyers: only Bedovy, Furor, Plutón and the Weihaiwei boats.
- No IJN case of surrender or scuttling in action 1890-1905, so there's no Japanese temper data from this period.
- Ammunition remaining at the decision is not given in any source fetched. Peter's condition (2) can't be checked against a single 1890-1905 case.
