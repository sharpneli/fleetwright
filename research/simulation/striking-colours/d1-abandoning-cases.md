## Q1. The case table: per ship, cause, mode of loss, time, conditions, rescue, numbers

### Takeaway
I collected 60 cases, about 30 of them with key figures read from sources this session. They split into three regimes. Ships that **blew up** lost 98-100% of the crew whatever the rescue. Ships that **capsized in under about 15 minutes** lost anywhere from 5% to 99%, depending almost wholly on whether rescue craft were within a few hundred metres. Ships that **sank slowly (more than about 1 hour)** saved 80-100% when friends could come alongside. They saved 0-25% when the enemy was still fighting, the sea was cold or rough, or nobody came.

### Inferences

## Q2. Synthesis: survivor share by mode of loss, time available, rescue and water temperature

### Takeaway
Two variables explain most of the spread: whether the ship **blew up**, and whether **rescue craft were within reach and free to act in the first 1-2 hours**. Time afloat matters mainly by letting rescuers arrive and letting boats and floats get away. Cold water, night and a rough sea then cut the share of men in the water who lived, often from most to a few percent.

### Inferences
Shares below are survivors over those aboard at the loss (men killed earlier in action included). They are grouped medians and ranges from the table, in round numbers **[INFERRED]**.

| Regime | Survivor share | Cases |
|---|---|---|
| Blew up at sea | 0-2% (median about 0.5%) | 12+ (QM, Indef, Invinc, Defence, BP, Pommern, Good Hope, Vanguard, Bulwark, Pathfinder 4%, S35, Kawachi lower) |

| More than 1 hour, no rescue (still under fire, alone, enemy steamed on) | 0-5% (Wiesbaden 0.2, Suvorov about 2 by early transfer, Cöln 0.2, Scharnhorst 0, Leipzig 6, Nürnberg 2) | 6 |

- **Time available** is a gate, not a slope. Under about 5 minutes almost no one gets off, beyond men already on deck. Hogue's men were ordered off within 5 minutes and she capsized at 10. Gambetta lowered two boats in 10 minutes. With 15-30 minutes most men get onto the water, but they need floats and rescuers. Over about 1 hour, a ship that is not under fire can be emptied onto other ships' decks (Lützow, Warrior, Audacious, Szent István). Getting off is necessary but not sufficient.
- **Rescue present**: with a rescuer alongside or within about 1 nm in the first 30 minutes, 80-95% lived. With none, 0-20% lived in temperate water, whatever the ship's size. Who rescued: the enemy rescued after a one-sided finish (Rurik, Ushakov, Svetlana, Gneisenau, Mainz, Nestor, Nomad, Turbulent). Friends rescued when the loss came outside a fight or the fight had moved on (Oslyabya under fire, Lützow, Warrior, Szent István, Triumph). Neutrals rescued late: Flora, Modesta, Vidar, a Norwegian steamer, a British merchant ship, Dutch trawlers. Nobody rescued when the winning side was still engaged or chasing (Scharnhorst, Monmouth, Good Hope, Alexander III, Wiesbaden, Jutland's night destroyers), or when weather barred it (Hampshire).
- **Water temperature and night**: in about 6-10 °C water, survival of men in the water fell to tens of percent within 1-2 hours. Blücher, Gneisenau (with deaths after pickup) and Formidable (the pinnace's 22 hours) fit this. The Leipzig and Nürnberg few fit it too. In 12-17 °C water, men lasted hours (the cruisers of 22 Sep, 3+ hours; Shark's raft, about 5 hours; Ardent, 5 hours). But at night and with no searcher, Navarin's about 70 dwindled to 3 by 16 hours, and Goliath at night lost 81%. In 20 °C and warmer water, nearly all who reached the water and were looked for were saved (Szent István, Rurik, Garibaldi). Those shares are proposed shapes for the model, not fits **[INFERRED]**.
- **List and the order**: in the four sourced cases the order (or the moment men were free to go) came at 7-20° of list, but ships capsized from that state within 10 minutes to about 2.5 hours. List at the order does not predict time left. Rate of flooding does: Gambetta from 15° capsized in about 10 minutes; Formidable from 20° counterflooded and lasted 2.4 hours; Szent István at 7-10° lasted about 2.8 hours **[INFERRED]**.
- **Ship size** matters little once the mode is set. A 26,000 t battlecruiser that blew up kept about 1%; a 935 t destroyer sunk at night kept 0-6%; a 700-crew battleship that capsized with help alongside kept 90%. In the slow-sinking cases a big ship's extra time and boats help. In the fast cases the bigger crew below decks hurts. Most of Hogue's and Cressy's deaths were men in the water, not men trapped **[INFERRED]**.
- This agrees with crews-under-fire.md §8: on lost ships, killed totals are dominated by the sinking and the water, not by the shells. Blücher about 70 heavy hits and about 800 dead; Wiesbaden and Scharnhorst wholesale.

- Sea state is a description, not a Beaufort number, in most sources.
